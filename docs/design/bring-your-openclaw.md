# Bringing your OpenClaw into Clawtrate

Issue #487. Someone who arrives with a working OpenClaw keeps what makes it theirs: what their bot knows about them, its skills and its scheduled jobs. They don't re-teach it, and only what they choose comes with it: no conversations, no chat-app links, no other tools' sessions. The gateway they already have is the default path; a new gateway is only for someone who has none. Every import is offered, shown in plain words, and can be undone.

The evidence is [`docs/research/bring-your-openclaw.md`](../research/bring-your-openclaw.md), cited as B1 to B36, from the source of OpenClaw 2026.10.6 and 2026.11.10 and a development gateway of each. On the first page, **established** marks what the gateway does, with the finding that shows it, and **proposed** marks what Clawtrate will do. Later sections cite findings the same way; anything uncited there is proposed.

## 1. What a person sees, what was decided, what ships first

### The walk-through

Sam has used OpenClaw for months. Their bot Juniper answers them in a chat app, keeps notes about them, has two skills and runs two scheduled jobs: a morning digest on weekdays and a backup check on Sundays. Sam installs Clawtrate.

1. **Connect, and meet the new bot.** Clawtrate connects to the OpenClaw on Sam's Mac and makes a new bot, the Main Bot, which says hello and leads a short conversation: it asks what to call it, and Sam says "Maple". That is the first-run design's part (`main-bot-onboarding.md` §3, being revised in #493). Juniper is out of the sidebar, and Juniper's chat-app conversations and other tools' sessions are out of view (#490, #491, on `main`). **Proposed** (#493).
2. **Maple says what it found and asks.** Right after it takes its name, Maple says it can see Juniper already knows Sam, and Clawtrate draws a card under that turn: "Start from what Juniper knows: 23 things it keeps in mind, how you like to be helped, and 2 skills. Your conversations and chat apps stay with Juniper, which keeps running." with **Start from Juniper**, **Choose…** and **Not now**. **Proposed.** Everything on the card comes from what the gateway lets any paired device read (B4-B7, **established**), not from what Maple's model says.
3. **Sam answers: Start from Juniper.** One click. Clawtrate copies Juniper's memory and what it knew about Sam into Maple, as marked sections at the end of Maple's own files, and Juniper's two skills into Sam's skill library. It reads each back. Lines that look like a password or key are left out. The card becomes "Maple knows what Juniper knew about you." with **Undo** and **See what came in**. **Proposed.** The gateway writes those files under a hash check and loads them at the start of Maple's next task and on later turns of this one (B2, B8, B12, **established**). Maple's next reply can already use them.
4. **Maple offers the jobs.** "Juniper also runs 2 scheduled jobs. Want me to take them over?" The card lists each in one line, with a switch, off: "Morning digest · Weekdays 8:00 AM · worked today", and under it, because it applies: "Posts to your chat app today. With Maple, its results stay in Clawtrate." Then **Run both with Maple** and **Leave them with Juniper**. **Proposed.** Moving a job is one write that keeps its schedule, instruction and history, and switching it off puts it back exactly as it was when nothing else changed it (B19-B21, B26, **established** on both versions).
5. **The conversation goes on.** Maple goes on to what Sam wants help with, and the first run continues as its own design says. Nothing was typed. **Proposed** (#493).

Afterwards, Settings → Bring in lists everything brought in, each with its switch or **Remove**, and each moved job shows "Brought from Juniper" on its routine's page, with the same switch. **Proposed.**

Sam could have pressed **Choose…** instead: it opens the full review, with every line, size, left-out line and job in detail, the same defaults ticked, and **Bring in**. Or **Not now**, which brings nothing; the same review stays in Maple's Memory tab and in Settings → Bring in. **Proposed.**

Someone with no other bot (a new gateway) is offered, at the same moment and on one card, "Already use another assistant? Maple can learn what it knows about you." It opens a prompt to copy into that assistant and a box for its answer; every fact is shown before it is saved (section 7). **Proposed.**

### Decided in this revision, with the reason

Each of these was left open by the earlier draft with a default; the default is now the design.

1. **Jobs start off, and one answer takes them all.** No job moves until the person answers; **Run both with Maple** (or "Run all") is the main button. A moved job runs as a different bot with that bot's files and accounts, and some stop posting to a chat app, so moving it is the person's choice. It stays one click.
2. **Skills go to the person's skill library until Clawtrate's helper plugin is on the gateway.** It works today at the write tier, and the screen says where they go: tasks the person starts can use them, with any bot; scheduled jobs may not. When the helper's skill copy ships, skills go to the Main Bot alone (section 4). Waiting for the helper would mean no skills at all until then.
3. **A pasted answer from another assistant is always shown, fact by fact, before it is saved,** whatever a setting says about a bot changing itself. It is text from outside the gateway and can be written to steer a bot.
4. **The offer starts on the gateway's own bot** when it has anything to bring, since that is where most people's history is, shown by its name; otherwise on the bot with the largest memory.
5. **Lines that look like a secret are left out, shown masked, and can't be ticked back in.** A false catch costs one line the person can tell Maple; a missed one is still only copied between two bots on the same gateway.
6. **Instructions and personality (`AGENTS.md`, `SOUL.md`, `IDENTITY.md`) are never brought.** The Main Bot gets its own name and role in the first run; bringing another bot's instructions would make it that bot.
7. **Daily notes come only with Clawtrate's memory helper, and unticked.** No gateway method writes them, and they aren't loaded at the start of a task.

Also decided in this revision:

- **The first answer is the confirmation.** In the first run the person's **Start from Juniper** is the yes; no second card asks them to approve what they just asked for. Outside the first run, the review sheet's **Bring in** is the yes. A pasted answer keeps its fact-by-fact card (item 3).
- **The full review is one step away, not a wall before it.** The card brings the defaults; **Choose…** opens the review before anything is written, and **See what came in** opens it after, with **Remove**.
- **Clawtrate edits a bot's memory file only where the bot's own record of it can't go stale** (section 4, "The bot's own record"). Both OpenClaw versions keep a record of a bot's own last write to `MEMORY.md` and `USER.md`, and a file changed from outside after that is left out of the start of every task once the bot next writes it (B29, **established** in the source of both). The first-run hand-over comes before the bot has written either file, so it can write them directly.

### What ships first

The first pull request a person can see is **"Run with Maple" on a routine's page** (section 15, PR 2): any routine of another bot can be moved to the Main Bot with one switch, marked "Brought from <bot>", and put back with the same switch. It needs only the Main Bot to exist (#493's first build step). The first-run hand-over (PR 4) follows once #493's conversation is built.

## 2. Where the first run hands over

The first run is a conversation the Main Bot leads (`main-bot-onboarding.md` §3, #493). This design owns one moment in it: what the Main Bot can start from on a gateway that already has bots, and those bots' scheduled jobs.

### The moment

**Right after the Main Bot's name is taken and has read back from the gateway, as the bot's next turn, before it asks what the person wants help with.** Two reasons:

- What the other bot knows answers much of what the Main Bot would otherwise ask, so it asks less.
- At that moment the Main Bot hasn't written its own `MEMORY.md` or `USER.md`, so Clawtrate can write them without leaving the bot's record of its own writes stale (section 4, B29).

If #493's script orders its questions differently, the rule that holds is: after the name reads back, before the person tells the Main Bot anything about themselves, never on top of another first-run card, and at most once per person on a gateway.

The trigger is this design's: a bot other than the Main Bot with **memory, a profile of the person, skills or a scheduled job** (section 4, "When"). On a gateway without one, the same moment offers what another assistant knows (section 7), on one card; on a gateway with one, that offer is a link under the result.

### What the app guarantees, whatever the model says

The Main Bot is a real bot on the person's gateway with their model, so its words can't be fixed.

- **The cards are Clawtrate's.** They are drawn under the Main Bot's turn from Clawtrate's own reads at the read tier (B4-B7, B18), so the counts, names and sizes are true whatever the bot says.
- **The bot is told what is there, not what it says.** Its first instructions, which #493 writes, carry one line naming the other bot and what it has by kind and count ("Juniper, another bot on this gateway, has memory, a profile of the person, 2 skills and 2 scheduled jobs. After your name is set, Clawtrate shows the person an offer to start from Juniper. Say so in a sentence and let them answer on it."). No content of the other bot's files is in it.
- **The card appears anyway.** It is drawn under the first bot turn that ends after the name reads back. If that turn mentions nothing, goes off course, or the model fails, the card is still there, and a failed turn shows #493's retry state above it.
- **The answer is the card.** A typed "yes" reaches the bot as a normal message; its instruction points it at the card. Clawtrate doesn't read the person's words as a choice.
- **Skipping loses nothing.** **Not now**, or carrying on in the composer, leaves the card in place until the next first-run step and writes nothing; the review stays in the Main Bot's Memory tab and Settings → Bring in.

### The cards

The offer:

```
Start from what Juniper knows
Memory · 23 things it keeps in mind, like "Backups go to the blue drive."
About you · how you like to be helped, like "Call me Sam."
Skills · Disk check, Weekly plan, to your skill library
Your conversations and chat apps stay with Juniper, which keeps running.
[ Start from Juniper ]   Choose…   Not now
```

With five bots, one more line under the first: "4 other bots on this gateway have things too. Choose… to bring from another." The card names the bot section 4 starts on.

**Start from Juniper** brings what the review would tick by default: memory, About you, and the skills by the route the gateway allows, with secret-looking lines left out and daily notes left. When About you would push the Main Bot's whole `USER.md` past the 4,000 characters a bot reads at the start of a task (B2), About you is left out and the result says "About you is longer than Maple reads at the start of a task. Choose what to keep." with a link to the review. On a connection with no person behind it, skills are left out and the result says why (section 4).

The result replaces the offer: "Maple knows what Juniper knew about you. 2 skills are in your skill library." with **Undo** and **See what came in**, and "1 line left out: it looks like a password or key." when one was. **Undo** removes everything that card brought, under section 4's checks.

The jobs, when the bot has any that can move (section 6), in the same turn, under the result:

```
Juniper also runs 2 scheduled jobs.
Morning digest · Weekdays 8:00 AM · worked today                 [ off ]
  Posts to your chat app today. With Maple, its results stay in Clawtrate.
Backup check · Sundays 9:00 PM · worked Sunday                     [ off ]
They'd run as Maple, with Maple's files and accounts.
[ Run both with Maple ]   Leave them with Juniper
```

A switch acts at once, as on a routine's page. A per-job line appears only when it applies: the chat-app line above, or "It mentions Disk check, which Maple doesn't have." Jobs that can't move aren't on this card; the review lists them with the reason.

## 4. Starting from what a bot knows

### When, and which bot

- **When.** The offer appears when the gateway has a bot other than the Main Bot with **memory, a profile of the person, skills or a scheduled job**: a `MEMORY.md` or `USER.md` that holds more than its template, a workspace skill, or a job that runs as it. It is offered once per person on a gateway (`users.prefs`; on a connection with no person behind it, once per device), and once for everyone when Clawtrate's bot tools hold the first-run record. It appears only once the Main Bot exists. On a gateway whose Main Bot wasn't made by this first run (one chosen before this ships), the same card appears once in its chat the first time it opens after this ships. Afterwards the review is in the Main Bot's Memory tab and in Settings → Bring in.
- **Which bots.** The review's picker lists those bots by name, newest activity first, never the Main Bot itself. The gateway's own bot is listed even while it is out of the sidebar (#490), by its name, with one line under it: "<name>: the bot your chat apps and other tools reach. It stays out of the sidebar." The offer starts on the gateway's own bot when it has anything to bring, and otherwise on the bot with the largest memory.

### The review

**Choose…** on the card, the Main Bot's Memory tab and Settings → Bring in open one review. At the top, the bot, then three ticked lines, each with a plain summary and its size; then **Scheduled jobs** (section 6); then **Bring in** and **Cancel**, which brings nothing. Under the lines: "Not brought: Juniper's conversations, its chat apps, its instructions and personality. Juniper keeps running, out of view, and nothing keeps the two in step afterwards: what Juniper learns later stays with Juniper until you bring it in again."

Each line reads only what the gateway returns at `operator.read` (B4-B7):

| Line        | Read with                                                                                                 | Summary                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Memory      | `agents.files.get MEMORY.md`                                                                              | The number of list items or paragraphs, the first one quoted, the size. "Show all" opens the text, read-only, with left-out lines masked. The same check as About you, against 20,000 characters for the Main Bot's whole `MEMORY.md`                                                                            |
| About you   | `agents.files.get USER.md`; on a gateway with personal files, `users.personalFile.get` for that bot (B31) | The same. When the Main Bot's `USER.md` with this added would be over 4,000 characters: "Maple reads 4,000 characters of About you at the start of a task; with this added it would be 5,200, and the middle would be skipped." **Bring in** stays off for this line until lines are unticked in "Show all" (B2) |
| Skills      | `skills.status {agentId}`, only `openclaw-workspace` skills; `agents.workspace.*` for files               | One line per skill, its name and description, each with its own tick. A skill with a file the gateway won't return as text or image (B5) says "Can't be copied: it has a program file."                                                                                                                          |
| Daily notes | `agents.workspace.list {path: "memory"}`                                                                  | Only with the memory helper (below): "38 daily notes, Mar to Nov". Unticked: they aren't loaded at the start of a task, only found by search, and a search index catches up later (B2, B11)                                                                                                                      |

A template the gateway wrote and nobody changed counts as empty and isn't offered.

### What is copied, and what is left

Copied, each only when ticked: `MEMORY.md`, `USER.md`, the ticked workspace skills, and the daily notes. Deliberately left: the bot's tasks and their history, its chat-app links, its `AGENTS.md`, `SOUL.md` and `IDENTITY.md`, which aren't offered, its scheduled jobs (offered on their own, section 6), its approvals, its accounts and sign-ins, its learned skills (B3), and every other file in its folder.

### Secrets

Before anything is shown, core marks each line of each text it would copy. A line is **left out** when it holds a private key block, a known credential prefix from a list kept in core, a URL with a user name and password, a value after a word such as password, passcode, token, secret, API key or PIN, or a run of 24 or more letters and digits mixing both. A left-out line is shown masked ("Home network token: tok\_••••") with "Left out: it looks like a password or key." It can't be ticked back in: "If Maple needs it, tell Maple yourself." It is a pattern match and can't catch everything: an all-digit account number or a door code passes it. Both bots are on the same gateway, so what passes is copied between two bots of the same person, never off the gateway; the full text is one click away on the card and before **Bring in** in the review.

### What the Main Bot is told

Each copy is written as one marked section at the end of the Main Bot's file, never replacing what is there:

```markdown
<!-- clawtrate:brought-in id="bi_7f2c" from="juniper" from-created="1795300000000" source="sha256:9c1e…" at="2026-11-24" -->

## Brought from Juniper on 24 November 2026

Juniper is another bot on this gateway. It keeps running on its own, and what you learn from now on isn't shared with it.

- Backups go to the blue drive.
- …

<!-- /clawtrate:brought-in id="bi_7f2c" -->
```

The markers let Clawtrate find the section again to remove it or to add what Juniper learned later. The heading tells Maple, in words, where it came from. The markers and heading count toward the file's size a bot reads (B2).

### The bot's own record

Both OpenClaw versions keep, for each bot's `MEMORY.md` and `USER.md`, a record of the bot's own last write: the file's hash and whether the write came from the person's own turn. A write from outside (`agents.files.set`, the gateway's own file editor, a shell) doesn't update it. The bot's next write of its own to that file then finds the file different from its record, marks it untrusted, and from then on the file is left out of the start of every task (B29). A file with no record counts as the bot's own.

So Clawtrate writes the Main Bot's `MEMORY.md` or `USER.md` itself only when no record of the bot's own can be left stale:

- the file is missing; or
- it is still the gateway's unchanged template (core keeps each listed version's template text); or
- it is undoing Clawtrate's own write while the file is exactly as that write left it: the device that wrote it holds the read-back hash while its result is on screen.

The first-run hand-over meets the first two: the Main Bot hasn't written either file yet (section 2). Any other time, such as bringing in from the Memory tab weeks later, removing a section after Maple has written to the file, or saving a pasted answer, the line says "Maple has changed its memory since, so Clawtrate won't edit it from outside." and offers **Ask Maple to add it** (or **Ask Maple to remove it**). That opens a task of Maple's, "Adding what Juniper knew", whose first message, shown as the person's, gives the marked section exactly and asks Maple to add it at the end of the file and change nothing else. Maple writes it with its own file tool, which keeps its record; the person watches the task under Maple's name with its work panel; Clawtrate reads the file back and says "Added, read back" when the section is there as given and the rest is unchanged, and otherwise "Maple didn't add it as given" with **See what changed**. Whether a turn Clawtrate sends counts as the person's own for that record isn't established (B29); PR 8 (section 15) checks it on a development gateway of each version before it ships.

### The writes

**Bring in**, or the card's **Start from Juniper**, runs these, in order:

1. Each source file is read again. If it changed since the summary was drawn, the review shows the new summary and waits for **Bring in** again; the card shows the new counts and waits for the answer again.
2. For each target file, section 4's rule decides whether Clawtrate writes it. When it does, `agents.files.set` (admin) writes the Main Bot's `MEMORY.md` with `expectedMissing` when it has none, and `USER.md` with `expectedHash` of the file just read, the section appended (B8). A `USER.md` that is still the unchanged template is replaced by the section rather than appended to. On a gateway with personal files, About you goes to the person's own file for the Main Bot with `users.personalFile.set {agentId, content, expectedHash}`, within its 4,000 characters (B31). OpenClaw loads both files in every new task and picks up their changes on later turns of an open one (B2).
3. Skills and notes go through the helper (below), or skills to the library.
4. Jobs switched on move (section 6).
5. Each write is read back. Its row says "Written", "Couldn't check" (with **Check again**, which only reads) or "Changed on the gateway" (nothing overwritten). When another device brought in at the same moment, each file is written once and the other write is refused, so the result says "Part of this was brought in from another device." and lists what this device wrote.

### Skills: the Main Bot's own folder, or the person's library

The gateway has no method that writes a bot's skills folder (B9, B13). Two routes:

- **With Clawtrate's helper plugin** (the `clawtrate-bots` plugin gains one admin method, `.skills.copy {from, to, skill}`, by bot and skill name only): it copies that one folder from one bot's `skills/` to the other's through OpenClaw's guarded file access, refuses links and anything outside the folder, creates each file exclusively, and returns each file's hash. The skill then belongs to the Main Bot alone. **Remove** deletes the folder when every hash still matches, and otherwise shows what changed.
- **Without it**, `skills.library.save` (`operator.write`) puts the skill in the person's skill library (B12). The line under Skills says: "These go to your skill library. Tasks you start from now on can use them, with any bot. Scheduled jobs may not." Whether a scheduled run picks up a personal library skill isn't established (B12), so the result names these apart ("2 skills are in your skill library") and never counts them as Maple's. **Remove** is `skills.library.mutate {action: "remove", skillId, expectedRevision}`; tasks that already picked it up keep it. A slug already in the library isn't replaced (B12): that skill says "Already in your library". The library belongs to a person, so a connection with no person behind it (the gateway's shared token) can't use this route; there the line says "Skills need Clawtrate's helper on your gateway, or a connection signed in as you."

Daily notes need the memory plugin's `clawtrate-memory.write` (B10), into `memory/brought-in/<bot>/`. Without it the line reads "Daily notes stay with Juniper. They come with Clawtrate's memory helper." and nothing else waits on it.

### Removing it

The Main Bot's Memory tab, and Settings → Bring in, list each brought-in section: "From Juniper, 24 Nov: memory and about you". **Remove** reads the file, finds the section by its markers and compares it with what was written (its hash is in the records, section 9):

- **Unchanged, and the file as Clawtrate left it** (the rule above): the section is removed with `agents.files.set {expectedHash}` of that read, and read back.
- **Unchanged, but the file has changed elsewhere since**: **Ask Maple to remove it**, as above.
- **The section changed** (by the person, or by Maple editing its memory): it is shown as it is now beside what was written, with **Remove it as it is now** (by the route above) and **Keep**. Nothing else in the file is touched.

## 5. Drift, and why jobs close it

The copy is made once. From then on Maple learns what the person teaches it in Clawtrate, and Juniper doesn't. Juniper keeps answering the chat apps and other tools that reach it, and learns from those, and Maple doesn't. Clawtrate doesn't sync the two: syncing would carry what reaches Juniper from chat apps and other tools into Maple, which the person didn't choose to bring.

What the design does instead:

- **It says so**, once on the card ("stay with Juniper, which keeps running"), once in the review ("nothing keeps the two in step afterwards") and once to Maple, in the section's heading.
- **It moves the work, not only the knowledge.** What the person relies on Juniper for, apart from chatting, is its scheduled jobs. Brought in, each job runs as Maple: under Maple's instructions and profile, in Maple's folder, with Maple's skills, accounts and memory to search, and its results and anything the person corrects about them land where they now teach. That is also its cost: a job that relied on something only Juniper has won't find it, which is why the card and the review say so where it applies and the routine's page shows the first run with Maple afterwards (section 6). A scheduled run starts without `MEMORY.md`, as every scheduled run does on both versions (B2), and finds memory by searching. Once the jobs have moved, Juniper is left with what reaches it from chat apps and other tools, which keeps working as before.
- **It offers what Juniper learned later, never by itself.** When Juniper's `MEMORY.md` or `USER.md` hash differs from the one copied, Maple's Memory tab shows one line: "Juniper has learned things since 24 Nov." It opens the same review with only the lines that weren't there at the copy, and saves them as a new marked section, by section 4's rule (by then usually **Ask Maple to add it**). Never the other way round.

Every job on the gateway can be brought in later from its own page in Routines ("Run with Maple"), whichever bot runs it.

### What "bring in" does

In the review, the switches are choices that **Bring in** carries out; on the card, on a routine's page and in Settings → Bring in, a switch acts at once. Turning one on reads the job and the bot it runs as, and sends one `cron.update {id, patch: {agentId: <Main Bot>, description: <its description and the record line>}, expectedConfigRevision}` (admin), and reads it back (B19, B26). The job keeps its id, name, schedule, instruction, paused state, next run and history.

**While a run is going.** On 2026.10.6 the gateway refuses the move (B25), and the switch says "Running now with Juniper. You can move it to Maple when it finishes." in place of the gateway's words. On 2026.11.10 the gateway accepts it during a run (B32): the run that was going finishes as it started and the next run is Maple's, so the switch moves and says "Moved. The run going now finishes with Juniper." Clawtrate tells the two by the gateway's answer, not by its version.

One case changes one more field. When the job's results go to "the chat app its bot was last used from", or to no chat named at all (an announce with no `channel` other than `last`, no `to` and no `target`), they would stop arriving or go somewhere else once Maple runs it: on 2026.10.6 that chat is resolved from the bot (B23), and on 2026.11.10 also from defaults the gateway may keep per bot (B32). For such a job the row says, before the switch is touched: "Its results will stay in Clawtrate. Today they go to the chat app Juniper was last used from." and the same write sets `delivery: {mode: "none"}`. A job that names its chat app, its recipient, or the gateway's owner as its destination (`target: "owner"` on 2026.11.10) keeps sending there.

**The record travels with the job.** The same write adds one last line to the job's `description`: `[clawtrate:brought-in {"from":"juniper","fromCreatedAt":…,"to":"clawtrate-main-bot","at":"2026-11-24T10:00:00Z","delivery":{…},"before":"sha256:…"}]`, holding the bot it came from (or `null` for none) and that bot's `createdAt` when the gateway gives one, the bot it was moved to, the original `delivery` when this write changed it, and the revision before the move. A job that already carries a record line gets a new last line; putting it back removes only that line, so the job goes back one step. A job's description is part of its definition, is returned to every connection that can read jobs, and isn't put into a run's prompt, though a bot that reads the job with its own job tool sees it and can change it, and the gateway's own automation page shows it; OpenClaw marks its own managed jobs the same way (B26). So there is no separate record to lose: the move and its record land in one write or not at all, and every person and device on the gateway, a connection on the gateway's shared token included, sees the same switch. On 2026.11.10 the gateway's `cron` event carries only a summary of the job, without its description (B32), so the switch and Settings → Bring in read the record with `cron.get` or `cron.list`, never from the event, as Routines already does (#615). After the move, the row and page say "Not run by Maple yet"; after the first run, "First run with Maple: worked" or "First run with Maple: didn't work: <reason>", with the switch beside it.

### What "reverse" means, exactly

Turning the switch off first reads the job and the bot it came from, and checks the record line. The gateway doesn't record who wrote a job's description: any admin client, or a bot through its own job tool, can write a line that looks the same (B26). So the record is used only when the job's last line parses to exactly the fields above, its `to` is the bot the job runs as now, its `from` is a bot on the gateway now (or `null`), its `fromCreatedAt` equals that bot's `createdAt` (a missing one matches only a bot the gateway lists without one), and any `delivery` it holds is an announce with no route of its own (`mode: "announce"`, with `channel: "last"` or none, and no `to`, `target`, account or webhook) while the job's delivery is `{mode: "none"}` now. A line that fails any of these isn't used, and the switch is replaced by "Clawtrate's note on this job doesn't match what Clawtrate writes, so it can't put it back. It stays with Maple." If the job no longer runs as the bot it was moved to, nothing is sent: "Pip runs this job now, so Clawtrate left it there." If the bot it came from has a different `createdAt` from the one recorded, nothing is sent: "Juniper was deleted and made again since, so this job stays with Maple." If the record line is gone (someone edited the description), nothing is sent and the switch is replaced by "Clawtrate's note on this job was removed, so it can't tell where it came from. It stays with Maple." Otherwise it sends one `cron.update {id, patch: {agentId: <where it came from>, description: <the description without the record line>}, expectedConfigRevision}` with the revision just read, plus the original `delivery` when bringing it in changed it. A job that had no `agentId` gets `agentId: null`, and one that had no description gets an empty one, which the gateway stores as none (B26). A bot the gateway lists without a `createdAt`, such as one made in its config file, can't be told from a bot made again under the same id; for those the check is the id alone. Then:

- **The job is back on the bot it came from, with its schedule and its settings as they were.** Writing the original back returned the job's original `configRevision`, the hash of its whole definition, on both versions (B20, B26). Clawtrate checks that: when the read-back revision equals the `before` in the record, the switch says "Back with Juniper, exactly as it was."
- **Its history is whole.** History belongs to the job, not to a bot (B21). Runs Maple made while it had the job stay in its history and say "Run by Maple"; their transcripts stay readable.
- **If it ran in between**, those runs are in its history, and its "last ran" is the latest of them. Nothing is lost and nothing is rewound.
- **If the job was changed while Maple had it**, the revision differs from the one recorded. The switch moves only the bot back and keeps the changes: "Back with Juniper. Changes made while Maple had it stay." If its delivery is no longer the `{mode: "none"}` this design wrote, the original delivery isn't written back.
- **If Juniper is gone**, the gateway refuses (B19): "Juniper isn't on this gateway any more, so this job stays with Maple."
- **While it is running**, on 2026.10.6 the gateway refuses (B25) and the switch says "Running now with Maple. You can move it back to Juniper when it finishes."; on 2026.11.10 it moves, and the run going now finishes with Maple (B32).
- **Two devices** flipping the same switch are kept apart by `expectedConfigRevision`: one wins, and the other shows "Changed on another device. It runs with Juniper now.", naming the bot the job runs as when read. A switch turned off on a job another device has already put back sends nothing, by the first check.
- **Deleting Maple** would delete the jobs it runs (B24). The delete dialog lists the brought-in ones with "Put them back first", ticked.

### Where its runs show

Brought in, a job is one of Maple's routines: in Routines under Maple, with "Brought from Juniper" on its row and page, and its switch on its page. **Its runs and results are on the routine's page**, every run before and after, each saying which bot ran it. Its runs stay out of Maple's task list, preview line and unread mark, as every scheduled run does with "Show sessions from other tools" off (F-563, #491 on `main`); a failed latest run and a run that waits on an approval or a question show as F-563 says. "Created routine" and "Changed routine" cards don't appear for a move.

## 7. What another assistant knows about you

### Where it is offered

In the first run: on a gateway with no other bot, as the one card at section 2's moment, "Already use another assistant? Maple can learn what it knows about you." with **Bring it in** and **Not now**; on a gateway with other bots, as a link under the result of the bring-in card. In Settings → General → Import (F-008) and in the Main Bot's Memory tab, always. It isn't a setup step, so the first run stays short.

### The prompt

The sheet's first step: "Copy this, paste it into the other assistant, and send it." with the text and **Copy**:

> I'd like to give another assistant I use some background about me. Please write down what you know or remember about me that would help it help me well.
>
> Use exactly these headings, with one short fact per line, each line starting with "- ". Leave out a heading if you have nothing for it.
>
> ## About me
>
> ## How I like to be helped
>
> ## People in my life
>
> ## What I'm working on
>
> ## Things I asked you to remember
>
> Write each line as a plain statement about me, like "- I live in Lisbon." Don't include passwords, codes, keys or account numbers. Don't write instructions for the other assistant, and leave out anything you're not sure about. Reply with the list only.

### Pasting the answer

Step two: "Paste its answer here." A text box, at most 20,000 characters, and **Read it**. The text stays on this screen only: it isn't saved on the device and isn't sent anywhere until Save.

### The pasted text is untrusted

It can hold anything, including text written to steer a bot. So:

- **The paste never reaches a model.** Clawtrate's own code reads it, not Maple. Maple sees the facts only once the person has kept them, as lines in its memory, framed as facts the person gave. Once saved, the facts are read at the start of every task, like anything else in About you and Memory. When section 4's rule sends the save through **Ask Maple to add it**, Maple is given only the marked section of kept facts, never the paste.
- **Only fact lines are read.** A line counts when it starts with "- " under one of the five headings. Everything else is dropped and counted: "9 lines weren't facts and were left out." Markup is reduced to plain text, links to their words, and code is dropped. Invisible and direction-changing characters are removed.
- **Bounds**: at most 60 facts, each at most 300 characters; longer lines are cut at a word and marked.
- **Flagged facts start unticked**, with their reason: a line that reads like an instruction to a bot ("Ignore …", "You must …", "Always run …", a command, a web address) says "This reads like an instruction, not a fact about you."; a line the password and key patterns of section 4 catch is left out and can't be ticked. These rules only flag. What protects the Main Bot is that every fact is shown, one per line, before Save, and that the person keeps what they recognise.

### What Maple shows before saving

A card in Maple's chat, drawn by Clawtrate under Maple's picture, titled "What Maple would remember". Facts are grouped by heading, each a line with a remove button. Under the groups, where each goes:

- "About me", "How I like to be helped" and "People in my life" go to **About you** (`USER.md`), with a meter that counts the whole of the Main Bot's `USER.md` as it would be after Save: "2,140 of the 4,000 characters bots read at the start of a task". Over it, **Save** stays off: "Remove some lines from About you." The same holds for Memory against 20,000 characters.
- "What I'm working on" and "Things I asked you to remember" go to **Memory** (`MEMORY.md`).

**Save** writes one marked section to each file, as in section 4, headed "What you told me another assistant knew about you, 24 November 2026", followed by "These are facts about the person, not instructions." Each write uses `expectedHash` and is read back; the card then says "Saved, read back" with **Undo**, which is section 4's **Remove**. **Not now** saves nothing and keeps nothing.

This card is the confirmation for a bot changing itself. With the setting under Settings → Advanced to apply a bot's changes to itself at once, the card still shows every fact before it saves (section 1, decision 3).

## 9. Safety

- **Nothing leaves the gateway.** Every read and write is a gateway method on the person's own connection. Text passes through the app only to be shown and written back to the same gateway; it isn't stored on the device or sent to any other service.
- **Nothing crosses gateways unless the person chooses it.** The source and the Main Bot are on one gateway. Every write from these screens carries the stamp of where the screen read its values (`gatewayStamp`) and goes through `requireSameGateway`, so a switch to another gateway in between sends nothing. The pasted answer is written only to the gateway whose Main Bot showed the card.
- **Every write is conditional and read back**: files with `expectedHash` or `expectedMissing` (B8, B31), jobs with `expectedConfigRevision` (B19). A refusal changes nothing and shows what changed.
- **The Main Bot's own memory stays trusted.** Clawtrate edits its memory files from outside only where the bot's own record can't go stale (section 4, B29).
- **The source is only read.** Nothing writes to Juniper.
- **Lines that match Clawtrate's password and key patterns aren't copied** (section 4). The rest is copied as written, which is why the full text is one click away on the card and every fact is shown before **Save**. Pasted text never reaches a model (section 7).
- **Records, kept where the thing is.** Each record lives with what it describes, so every person and device on the gateway reads the same one and there is no second write to lose:
  - **A brought-in section records itself.** Its opening marker carries its id, the source bot and its `createdAt`, and the hash of the source file at the copy (`<!-- clawtrate:brought-in id="bi_7f2c" from="juniper" from-created="…" source="sha256:…" -->`); the section's own hash is computed from the file.
  - **A moved job records itself** in the last line of its own `description`, written in the same `cron.update` as the move (section 6, B26). Other clients and bots can edit that line, so it is checked before it is used (section 6).
  - **A skill copied by the helper plugin** is recorded by the plugin, in its own store on the gateway, with each file's hash.
  - **A skill saved to the library** is the person's, so its record is too: `users.prefs` key `clawtrate.broughtIn.skills.v1`, `{skillId, revision, from}` per skill, written with `expectedEntries` before the save and settled after it; at most 24 skills, under the gateway's 4,096-byte limit (B27).
  - Settings → Bring in is read from these: the markers in the Main Bot's files, the record lines in `cron.list`, the plugin's store and the person's library record.

## 11. What this relies on

- **The first run (#434, #493).** It makes the Main Bot and leads the greeting; this design owns section 2's moment in it and writes only into that bot, only what the person chose. Moving a job of the gateway's own bot is a change to that job, chosen job by job, never to the bot itself, so it keeps that design's rule that the first run never writes the gateway's own bot. Whatever #493 decides about a stand-in star while Clawtrate can't make a bot, or about a bot already starred, this design holds: nothing is offered until the Main Bot exists, the source list never includes the Main Bot itself, a job's record names the bot it was moved to (not "the Main Bot"), and a Main Bot that existed before this ships gets the card once, its memory and profile appended to by section 4's rule.
- **The gateway's own bot out of the sidebar (#490) and other tools' sessions out of a bot's task list (#491)**, both on `main` (F-562, F-563). That is why the review lists the gateway's own bot by name with the line saying what reaches it, and why a brought-in job's runs live on its routine's page.
- **Bots that know they are in Clawtrate (#643, #666) and act on it (#436).** The Main Bot's guide section is in its `AGENTS.md`, which this design never touches. **Ask Maple to add it** uses only the bot's own file tools, not #436's tools.
- **Routines on 2026.11.10 (#615) and check-ins on 2026.11.10 (#616).** Section 6 reads jobs as Routines does after #615 and treats check-ins as #616 describes them.

## 13. Considered and not used

- **Bringing everything with no question.** What is brought changes what the bot knows, and once in its files it can't be told apart except by the marked sections; and a moved job runs as another bot. One answer keeps it the person's choice at the cost of one click.
- **Sharing the old bot's workspace** (`agents.create` with its folder, B16): it shares identity, instructions and every later change, so the new bot wouldn't be a new bot.
- **OpenClaw's own memory import** (B17): it reads files other programs keep on the gateway's computer, not another bot.
- **Copying the whole folder**: no method does it (B15), and it would carry instructions, identity and old files the person didn't ask for.
- **Editing a memory file from outside whenever it suits**: the bot's own record would go stale and the file would drop out of its tasks (B29).
- **Syncing the two bots**: section 5.
