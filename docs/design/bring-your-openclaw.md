# Bringing your OpenClaw into Clawtrate

Issue #487. Someone who arrives with a working OpenClaw keeps what makes it theirs: what their bot knows about them, its skills and its scheduled jobs. They don't re-teach it, and they don't get its clutter: no conversations, no chat-app links, no other tools' sessions. The gateway they already have is the default path; a new gateway is only for someone who has none. Clawtrate doesn't ask before bringing knowledge in: it brings it, shows what it brought, and offers Undo. A scheduled job moves only when the person flips its switch.

The evidence is [`docs/research/bring-your-openclaw.md`](../research/bring-your-openclaw.md), cited as B1 to B36, from the source of OpenClaw 2026.10.6 and 2026.11.10 and a development gateway of each. On the first page, **established** marks what the gateway does, with the finding that shows it, and **proposed** marks what Clawtrate will do. Later sections cite findings the same way; anything uncited there is proposed.

## 1. What a person sees, what was decided, what ships first

### The walk-through

Sam has used OpenClaw for months. Their bot Juniper answers them in a chat app, keeps notes about them, has two skills and runs two scheduled jobs: a morning digest on weekdays and a backup check on Sundays. Sam installs Clawtrate.

1. **Connect.** Clawtrate connects to the OpenClaw on Sam's Mac and makes a new bot, the Main Bot. Juniper is out of the sidebar, and Juniper's chat-app conversations and other tools' sessions are out of view (#490, #491, on `main`). **Proposed** (#493's first run).
2. **The new bot already knows Sam.** As it makes the Main Bot, Clawtrate copies Juniper's memory and what Juniper knew about Sam into the new bot's own files, as marked sections, and Juniper's two skills into Sam's skill library. Lines that look like a password or key are left out. **Proposed.** The gateway lets any paired device read another bot's files and skills, writes the new bot's files under a hash check, and loads them at the start of the new bot's first task (B2, B4-B8, B12, **established**).
3. **It says so in its greeting.** The new bot says hello and leads the conversation (#493). Its first instructions tell it what was brought in, so it can say so in its own words ("I've taken in what Juniper knew about you, Sam"). Under that turn, Clawtrate's own line: "Started from what Juniper knows: memory, About you, and 2 skills in your skill library. Your conversations and chat apps stay with Juniper." with **Undo** and **See what came in**. **Proposed.** Nothing to read and agree to, and nothing typed.
4. **It takes its name.** The bot asks what to call it; Sam says "Maple" and the name is taken (#493). **Proposed.**
5. **The jobs, each with a switch.** Under Maple's next turn, Clawtrate lists Juniper's jobs, one line each, with a switch, off: "Morning digest · Weekdays 8:00 AM · worked today", and where it applies: "Posts to your chat app today. With Maple, its results would stay in Clawtrate." Under the list, "Run all with Maple". Sam can flip one, flip all, or carry on talking; the list waits. **Proposed.** Moving a job is one write that keeps its schedule, instruction and history, and switching it back off puts it back exactly as it was when nothing else changed it (B19-B21, B26, **established** on both versions).
6. **The conversation goes on.** Maple goes on to what Sam wants help with, as the first-run design says. **Proposed** (#493).

Clicks: none to bring the knowledge in; one to move every job ("Run all with Maple"), or none to leave them. Afterwards, Settings → Bring in and Maple's Memory tab list everything brought in, each with **Remove** or its switch, on every device, and each moved job shows "Brought from Juniper" on its routine's page with the same switch. **Proposed.**

Someone with no other bot (a new gateway) gets one line under the greeting instead: "Bring what another assistant knows about you". It opens a prompt to copy into that assistant and a box for its answer; the facts in the answer are saved at once and shown with Undo (section 7). **Proposed.**

### What each step is

Every step of this design is one of three kinds: done at once and shown with Undo; not done, and said plainly; or destructive, which asks once. There is no fourth kind and no setting that turns asking on.

**Why jobs don't move by themselves.** Switching a job off puts its definition back exactly: the original revision comes back, and its history from both bots stays (B20, B21, B26). What can't be put back is what it did in between. A job brought in at setup would next run at 8:00 tomorrow, as Maple, with Maple's files and accounts; a job that relied on something only Juniper has would fail, and a digest that used to arrive in Sam's chat app would stay in Clawtrate. Sam would likely notice a day later, when the digest didn't arrive where they read it. That is a change of something that already works, which keeps acting after setup and is easy not to notice, so it isn't done unless Sam flips the switch. A switch is not a prompt: the list doesn't stop the conversation, and ignoring it leaves every job where it was.

### Decided in this revision, with the reason

Each of these was left open by the earlier draft with a default; the default is now the design, or the rule above replaced it.

1. **Jobs move only by their switch, which starts off; "Run all with Maple" flips them all.** The reason is above.
2. **Skills go to the person's skill library until Clawtrate's helper plugin is on the gateway.** It works today at the write tier, and the line says where they went: tasks the person starts can use them, with any bot; scheduled jobs may not. When the helper's skill copy ships, skills go to the Main Bot alone (section 4). Waiting for the helper would mean no skills at all until then.
3. **A pasted answer is saved at once, without a fact-by-fact review.** Only fact lines under the prompt's headings are read; lines that read like an instruction, a link or a command, secret-looking lines, and anything past the bounds are not saved, and the result says how many. What was saved is shown, with Undo for all of it and a remove button per line (section 7).
4. **Clawtrate brings from the gateway's own bot** when it has anything to bring, since that is where most people's history is, shown by its name; otherwise from the bot with the largest memory. With several such bots, the line says how many others have something and links to the review to bring from another.
5. **Lines that look like a secret are left out, shown masked in the review, and can't be added back.** A false catch costs one line the person can tell Maple; a missed one is still only copied between two bots on the same gateway.
6. **Instructions and personality (`AGENTS.md`, `SOUL.md`, `IDENTITY.md`) are never brought.** The Main Bot gets its own name and role in the first run; bringing another bot's instructions would make it that bot.
7. **Daily notes come only with Clawtrate's memory helper.** No gateway method writes them, and they aren't loaded at the start of a task.

Also decided in this revision:

- **Knowledge comes in when the Main Bot is made, before its first turn** (section 2), so its greeting can use it and its own memory record can't be left stale (B29).
- **The full review is a place, not a step.** "See what came in", the Main Bot's Memory tab and Settings → Bring in open it: every line, size and left-out line, to bring from another bot or remove a part.
- **Clawtrate edits a bot's memory file from outside only where the bot's own record of it can't go stale** (section 4, "The bot's own record"). Both OpenClaw versions keep a record of a bot's own last write to `MEMORY.md` and `USER.md`, and a file changed from outside after that is left out of the start of every task once the bot next writes it (B29, **established** in the source of both).

### What ships first

The first pull request a person can see is **"Run with Maple" on a routine's page** (section 15, PR 2): any routine of another bot can be moved to the Main Bot with one switch, marked "Brought from <bot>", and put back with the same switch. It needs only the Main Bot to exist (#493's first build step). The first-run bring-in (PR 4) follows once #493's conversation is built.

## 2. Where the first run hands over

The first run is a conversation the Main Bot leads (`main-bot-onboarding.md` §3, #493). This design owns two points in it: what the Main Bot starts from on a gateway that already has bots, and those bots' scheduled jobs.

### Knowledge: when the Main Bot is made, before its first turn

**Clawtrate brings knowledge in after `agents.create` has made the Main Bot and its identity file is written, and before the first message of its first task is sent.** Three reasons:

- The Main Bot's `MEMORY.md` is missing and its `USER.md` is the gateway's template, and the bot hasn't written either, so Clawtrate's writes can't leave the bot's record of its own writes stale (section 4, B29).
- Its first task loads both files at its start (B2), so the greeting already knows the person and asks less.
- Nothing waits on the person.

The other bots' files and skills are read while setup finishes (B4-B7), so the writes add about one round trip per file before the first message. If they haven't read back within 3 seconds, the first message is sent without waiting; the bot picks up the files on its next turn (B2), and the line appears under the turn in which they read back.

The trigger is this design's: a bot other than the Main Bot with **memory, a profile of the person, skills or a scheduled job** (section 4, "When"). It is done once, for the Main Bot the first run makes; a second device or person on the gateway finds it done. On a gateway without such a bot, nothing is brought, and the greeting's turn gets section 7's line.

### Jobs: after the name is taken

**The jobs list is drawn under the Main Bot's first turn that ends after its name has read back**, so its switches name the bot ("Run with Maple"). It is drawn once and stays in the conversation; the same switches are on each routine's page.

### What the app guarantees, whatever the model says

The Main Bot is a real bot on the person's gateway with their model, so its words can't be fixed.

- **The lines are Clawtrate's.** The "Started from" line and the jobs list are drawn from Clawtrate's own reads and read-backs (B4-B8, B18), under the bot's turns, so the counts and names are true whatever the bot says.
- **The bot is told what was done, not what to say.** Its first instructions, which #493 writes, carry one line naming the other bot and what was brought by kind and count, once it has read back ("Clawtrate added what Juniper, another bot on this gateway, knew about the person to your memory and profile, under 'Brought from Juniper'. Juniper's 2 scheduled jobs are listed for the person to move to you if they want. Mention this in a sentence."). No content of the other bot's files is in that line; the content is in the bot's own files.
- **If the model says nothing about it, goes off course or fails,** the line is still there, and a failed turn shows #493's retry state above it.
- **A typed request is a request.** "Take over the morning digest" reaches the bot as a normal message. With #436's tools the bot can flip that job's switch itself, as the person asked; until then it points at the list.

### The lines

Under the greeting's turn:

```
Started from what Juniper knows: memory, About you, and 2 skills in your skill library.
Your conversations and chat apps stay with Juniper, which keeps running.
1 line left out: it looks like a password or key.                 Undo · See what came in
```

With five bots, one more line: "4 other bots on this gateway have things too. Bring from another…", which opens the review. A part that wasn't brought says so on its own line (the table in section 1). **Undo** removes everything that line brought, under section 4's checks, from any device, for as long as the sections are there.

Under the Main Bot's turn after its name:

Morning digest · Weekdays 8:00 AM · worked today                 [ off ]

Backup check · Sundays 9:00 PM · worked Sunday                     [ off ]

A switch acts at once, as on a routine's page. A per-job line appears only when it applies: the chat-app line above, or "It mentions Disk check, which Maple doesn't have." Jobs that can't move aren't in this list; the review lists them with the reason.

## 4. Starting from what a bot knows

### When, and which bot

- **When.** In the first run, when the gateway has a bot other than the Main Bot with **memory, a profile of the person, skills or a scheduled job**: a `MEMORY.md` or `USER.md` that holds more than its template, a workspace skill, or a job that runs as it. Done once, for the Main Bot the first run makes (section 2). On a gateway whose Main Bot wasn't made by this first run (one chosen before this ships), nothing is copied into a bot that already has its own memory; its chat shows once "Juniper has memory and 2 scheduled jobs you can bring to this bot. Bring in…", which opens the review. Afterwards the review is in the Main Bot's Memory tab and in Settings → Bring in.
- **Which bot.** The gateway's own bot when it has anything to bring, listed by its name even while it is out of the sidebar (#490), and otherwise the bot with the largest memory; never the Main Bot itself. The review's picker lists every such bot by name, newest activity first, with the gateway's own bot's line under it: "<name>: the bot your chat apps and other tools reach. It stays out of the sidebar."

### What is brought by default

Memory, About you and every workspace skill, each by the rules below. Daily notes, the bot's tasks and their history, its chat-app links, its `AGENTS.md`, `SOUL.md` and `IDENTITY.md`, its approvals, its accounts and sign-ins, its learned skills (B3) and every other file in its folder are not. Its scheduled jobs are listed with switches (section 6).

### The review

"See what came in", "Bring from another…", the Main Bot's Memory tab and Settings → Bring in open one review. At the top, the bot; then a line per part, each with a plain summary and its size, ticked when it is in, with its **Remove** when it came in; then **Scheduled jobs** (section 6). Under the parts: "Not brought: Juniper's conversations, its chat apps, its instructions and personality. Juniper keeps running, out of view, and nothing keeps the two in step afterwards: what Juniper learns later stays with Juniper until you bring it in again." Choosing another bot and **Bring in** brings what is ticked from that bot, at once, by the rules below.

Each line reads only what the gateway returns at `operator.read` (B4-B7):

| Line        | Read with                                                                                                 | Summary                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Memory      | `agents.files.get MEMORY.md`                                                                              | The number of list items or paragraphs, the first one quoted, the size. "Show all" opens the text, read-only, with left-out lines masked. Checked against 20,000 characters for the Main Bot's whole `MEMORY.md`                                                                                                   |
| About you   | `agents.files.get USER.md`; on a gateway with personal files, `users.personalFile.get` for that bot (B31) | The same. When the Main Bot's `USER.md` with this added would be over 4,000 characters, it isn't brought: "Maple reads 4,000 characters of About you at the start of a task; with this added it would be 5,200, and the middle would be skipped." Unticking lines in "Show all" until it fits brings the rest (B2) |
| Skills      | `skills.status {agentId}`, only `openclaw-workspace` skills; `agents.workspace.*` for files               | One line per skill, its name and description. A skill with a file the gateway won't return as text or image (B5) isn't brought: "Can't be copied: it has a program file."                                                                                                                                          |
| Daily notes | `agents.workspace.list {path: "memory"}`                                                                  | Only with the memory helper (below): "38 daily notes, Mar to Nov", unticked: they aren't loaded at the start of a task, only found by search, and a search index catches up later (B2, B11)                                                                                                                        |

A template the gateway wrote and nobody changed counts as empty and isn't brought.

### Secrets

Before anything is written, core marks each line of each text it would copy. A line is **left out** when it holds a private key block, a known credential prefix from a list kept in core, a URL with a user name and password, a value after a word such as password, passcode, token, secret, API key or PIN, or a run of 24 or more letters and digits mixing both. The "Started from" line counts them ("1 line left out: it looks like a password or key."), and the review shows each masked ("Home network token: tok\_••••") with "If Maple needs it, tell Maple yourself." It can't be added back from the review. It is a pattern match and can't catch everything: an all-digit account number or a door code passes it. Both bots are on the same gateway, so what passes is copied between two bots of the same person, never off the gateway, and is one click from being seen and removed.

### What the Main Bot is told

Each copy is written as one marked section at the end of the Main Bot's file, never replacing what is there:

```markdown
<!-- clawtrate:brought-in id="bi_7f2c" from="juniper" from-created="1795300000000" source="sha256:9c1e…" rest="sha256:e3b0…" by="clawtrate" at="2026-11-24" -->

## Brought from Juniper on 24 November 2026

Juniper is another bot on this gateway. It keeps running on its own, and what you learn from now on isn't shared with it.

- Backups go to the blue drive.
- …

<!-- /clawtrate:brought-in id="bi_7f2c" -->
```

The markers let Clawtrate find the section again to remove it or to add what Juniper learned later. `rest` is the hash of the rest of the file, everything outside the section, as Clawtrate wrote it, and `by` says whether Clawtrate or the Main Bot wrote the section (below). The heading tells Maple, in words, where it came from. The markers and heading count toward the file's size a bot reads (B2).

### The bot's own record

Both OpenClaw versions keep, for each bot's `MEMORY.md` and `USER.md`, a record of the bot's own last write: the file's hash and whether the write came from the person's own turn. A write from outside (`agents.files.set`, the gateway's own file editor, a shell) doesn't update it. The bot's next write of its own to that file then finds the file different from its record, marks it untrusted, and from then on the file is left out of the start of every task (B29). A file with no record counts as the bot's own.

So Clawtrate writes the Main Bot's `MEMORY.md` or `USER.md` itself only when no record of the bot's own can be left stale:

- the file is missing; or
- it is still the gateway's unchanged template (core keeps each listed version's template text); or
- it is exactly as Clawtrate's own earlier write left it: every brought-in section in it says `by="clawtrate"`, each section is as written, and the rest of the file hashes to the section's `rest`. Then the bot hasn't written the file since, and before Clawtrate's first write it had no record. Any device can check this, after a restart too.

The first run's bring-in meets the first two (section 2), and its **Undo** meets the third until the Main Bot writes the file. Any other time, such as bringing in from the Memory tab weeks later, removing a section after Maple has written to the file, or saving a pasted answer, Clawtrate doesn't edit the file from outside: the Main Bot does it. Clawtrate opens a task of Maple's, "Adding what Juniper knew" (or "Removing what came from Juniper"), whose first message, shown as the person's, gives the marked section exactly, with `by="maple"`, and asks Maple to add it at the end of the file, or to remove that section, and change nothing else. Maple writes with its own file tool, which keeps its record; the task shows under Maple's name with its work panel; Clawtrate reads the file back and shows "Added, read back" when the section is there as given and the rest is unchanged, and otherwise "Maple didn't add it as given" with **See what changed**. Whether a turn Clawtrate sends counts as the person's own for that record isn't established (B29); PR 8 (section 15) checks it on a development gateway of each version before it ships. Until it does, the review says "Maple has changed its memory since. Tell Maple what to keep." and nothing is written from outside.

### The writes

The first run, and the review's **Bring in**, run these, in order:

1. Each source file is read again. If it changed since it was summarised, the summary is redrawn from the new read.
2. For each target file, the rule above decides who writes it. When Clawtrate does, `agents.files.set` (admin) writes the Main Bot's `MEMORY.md` with `expectedMissing` when it has none, and `USER.md` with `expectedHash` of the file just read, the section appended (B8). A `USER.md` that is still the unchanged template is replaced by the section rather than appended to. On a gateway with personal files, About you goes to the person's own file for the Main Bot with `users.personalFile.set {agentId, content, expectedHash}`, within its 4,000 characters (B31). OpenClaw loads both files in every new task and picks up their changes on later turns of an open one (B2).
3. Skills and notes go through the helper (below), or skills to the library.
4. Each write is read back. The line says "Started from what Juniper knows" only for what read back; a part that didn't says "Couldn't check About you" with **Check again**, which only reads, or "About you changed on the gateway; nothing was overwritten." When another device brought in at the same moment, each file is written once and the other write is refused, so the line says "Part of this was brought in from another device."

### Skills: the Main Bot's own folder, or the person's library

The gateway has no method that writes a bot's skills folder (B9, B13). Two routes:

- **With Clawtrate's helper plugin** (the `clawtrate-bots` plugin gains one admin method, `.skills.copy {from, to, skill}`, by bot and skill name only): it copies that one folder from one bot's `skills/` to the other's through OpenClaw's guarded file access, refuses links and anything outside the folder, creates each file exclusively, and returns each file's hash. The skill then belongs to the Main Bot alone. **Remove** deletes the folder when every hash still matches, and otherwise shows what changed and asks, since it would delete files Clawtrate didn't write.
- **Without it**, `skills.library.save` (`operator.write`) puts the skill in the person's skill library (B12). The line says "2 skills in your skill library", and the review: "Tasks you start from now on can use them, with any bot. Scheduled jobs may not." Whether a scheduled run picks up a personal library skill isn't established (B12), so these are never counted as Maple's. **Remove** is `skills.library.mutate {action: "remove", skillId, expectedRevision}`; tasks that already picked it up keep it. A slug already in the library isn't replaced (B12): that skill says "Already in your library". The library belongs to a person, so a connection with no person behind it (the gateway's shared token) can't use this route; there skills aren't brought and the line says "Skills need Clawtrate's helper on your gateway, or a connection signed in as you."

Daily notes need the memory plugin's `clawtrate-memory.write` (B10), into `memory/brought-in/<bot>/`. Without it the review reads "Daily notes stay with Juniper. They come with Clawtrate's memory helper." and nothing else waits on it.

### Removing it

**Undo** on the "Started from" line, and **Remove** in the Main Bot's Memory tab and Settings → Bring in ("From Juniper, 24 Nov: memory and about you"), read the file, find the section by its markers and compare it with what was written:

- **The section is as written and the file is as Clawtrate left it** (the rule above): the section is removed with `agents.files.set {expectedHash}` of that read, and read back. Done at once.
- **The section is as written, but the file has changed elsewhere since**: Maple removes it, as above. Done at once.
- **The section itself changed** (by the person, or by Maple editing its memory): removing it would delete text Clawtrate didn't write, so this one asks: the section as it is now beside what was written, with **Remove it as it is now** and **Keep**. Nothing else in the file is touched.

## 5. Drift, and why jobs close it

The copy is made once. From then on Maple learns what the person teaches it in Clawtrate, and Juniper doesn't. Juniper keeps answering the chat apps and other tools that reach it, and learns from those, and Maple doesn't. Clawtrate doesn't sync the two: syncing would carry what reaches Juniper from chat apps and other tools into Maple, which the person didn't choose to bring.

What the design does instead:

- **It says so**, once in the "Started from" line ("stay with Juniper, which keeps running"), once in the review ("nothing keeps the two in step afterwards") and once to Maple, in the section's heading.
- **It moves the work, not only the knowledge.** What the person relies on Juniper for, apart from chatting, is its scheduled jobs. Brought in, each job runs as Maple: under Maple's instructions and profile, in Maple's folder, with Maple's skills, accounts and memory to search, and its results and anything the person corrects about them land where they now teach. That is also its cost: a job that relied on something only Juniper has won't find it, which is why the jobs list and the review say so where it applies, why a job moves only by its switch, and the routine's page shows the first run with Maple afterwards (section 6). A scheduled run starts without `MEMORY.md`, as every scheduled run does on both versions (B2), and finds memory by searching. Once the jobs have moved, Juniper is left with what reaches it from chat apps and other tools, which keeps working as before.
- **It offers what Juniper learned later, never by itself.** When Juniper's `MEMORY.md` or `USER.md` hash differs from the one copied, Maple's Memory tab shows one line: "Juniper has learned things since 24 Nov." It opens the same review with only the lines that weren't there at the copy, and saves them as a new marked section, by section 4's rule (by then usually **Ask Maple to add it**). Never the other way round.

Every job on the gateway can be brought in later from its own page in Routines ("Run with Maple"), whichever bot runs it.

### What "bring in" does

A switch acts at once wherever it is: the first run's list, the review, a routine's page and Settings → Bring in. Turning one on reads the job and the bot it runs as, and sends one `cron.update {id, patch: {agentId: <Main Bot>, description: <its description and the record line>}, expectedConfigRevision}` (admin), and reads it back (B19, B26). The job keeps its id, name, schedule, instruction, paused state, next run and history.

**While a run is going.** On 2026.10.6 the gateway refuses the move (B25), and the switch says "Running now with Juniper. You can move it to Maple when it finishes." in place of the gateway's words. On 2026.11.10 the gateway accepts it during a run (B32): the run that was going finishes as it started and the next run is Maple's, so the switch moves and says "Moved. The run going now finishes with Juniper." Clawtrate tells the two by the gateway's answer, not by its version.

One case changes one more field. When the job's results go to "the chat app its bot was last used from", or to no chat named at all (an announce with no `channel` other than `last`, no `to` and no `target`), they would stop arriving or go somewhere else once Maple runs it: on 2026.10.6 that chat is resolved from the bot (B23), and on 2026.11.10 also from defaults the gateway may keep per bot (B32). For such a job the row says, before the switch is touched: "Its results will stay in Clawtrate. Today they go to the chat app Juniper was last used from." and the same write sets `delivery: {mode: "none"}`. A job that names its chat app, its recipient, or the gateway's owner as its destination (`target: "owner"` on 2026.11.10) keeps sending there.

**The record travels with the job.** The same write adds one last line to the job's `description`: `[clawtrate:brought-in {"from":"juniper","fromCreatedAt":…,"to":"clawtrate-main-bot","at":"2026-11-24T10:00:00Z","delivery":{…},"before":"sha256:…"}]`, holding the bot it came from (or `null` for none) and that bot's `createdAt` when the gateway gives one, the bot it was moved to, the original `delivery` when this write changed it, and the revision before the move. A job that already carries a record line gets a new last line; putting it back removes only that line, so the job goes back one step. A job's description is part of its definition, is returned to every connection that can read jobs, and isn't put into a run's prompt, though a bot that reads the job with its own job tool sees it and can change it, and the gateway's own automation page shows it; OpenClaw marks its own managed jobs the same way (B26). So there is no separate record to lose: the move and its record land in one write or not at all, and every person and device on the gateway, a connection on the gateway's shared token included, sees the same switch. On 2026.11.10 the gateway's `cron` event carries only a summary of the job, without its description (B32), so the switch and Settings → Bring in read the record with `cron.get` or `cron.list`, never from the event, as Routines already does (#615). After the move, the row and page say "Not run by Maple yet"; after the first run, "First run with Maple: worked" or "First run with Maple: didn't work: <reason>", with the switch beside it.

### What "reverse" means, exactly

Turning the switch off first reads the job and the bot it came from, and checks the record line. The gateway doesn't record who wrote a job's description: any admin client, or a bot through its own job tool, can write a line that looks the same (B26). So the record is used only when the job's last line parses to exactly the fields above, its `to` is the bot the job runs as now, its `from` is a bot on the gateway now (or `null`), its `fromCreatedAt` equals that bot's `createdAt` (a missing one matches only a bot the gateway lists without one), and any `delivery` it holds is an announce with no route of its own (`mode: "announce"`, with `channel: "last"` or none, and no `to`, `target`, account or webhook) while the job's delivery is `{mode: "none"}` now. A line that fails any of these isn't used, and the switch is replaced by "Clawtrate's note on this job doesn't match what Clawtrate writes, so it can't put it back. It stays with Maple." If the job no longer runs as the bot it was moved to, nothing is sent: "Pip runs this job now, so Clawtrate left it there." If the bot it came from has a different `createdAt` from the one recorded, nothing is sent: "Juniper was deleted and made again since, so this job stays with Maple." If the record line is gone (someone edited the description), nothing is sent and the switch is replaced by "Clawtrate's note on this job was removed, so it can't tell where it came from. It stays with Maple." Otherwise it sends one `cron.update {id, patch: {agentId: <where it came from>, description: <the description without the record line>}, expectedConfigRevision}` with the revision just read, plus the original `delivery` when bringing it in changed it. A job that had no `agentId` gets `agentId: null`, and one that had no description gets an empty one, which the gateway stores as none (B26). A bot the gateway lists without a `createdAt`, such as one made in its config file, can't be told from a bot made again under the same id; for those the check is the id alone. Then:

- **The job is back on the bot it came from, with its schedule and its settings as they were.** Writing the original back returned the job's original `configRevision`, the hash of its whole definition, on both versions (B20, B26). Clawtrate checks that: when the read-back revision equals the `before` in the record, the switch says "Back with Juniper, exactly as it was."
- **Its history is whole.** History belongs to the job, not to a bot (B21). Runs Maple made while it had the job stay in its history and say "Run by Maple"; their transcripts stay readable.
- **If it ran in between**, those runs are in its history, and its "last ran" is the latest of them. Nothing is lost and nothing is rewound.
- **If the job was changed while Maple had it**, the revision differs from the one recorded. The switch moves only the bot back and keeps the changes: "Back with Juniper. Changes made while Maple had it stay." If its delivery is no longer the `{mode: "none"}` this design wrote, the original delivery isn't written back.
- **If Juniper is gone**, the gateway refuses (B19): "Juniper isn't on this gateway any more, so this job stays with Maple."
- **While it is running**, on 2026.10.6 the gateway refuses (B25) and the switch says "Running now with Maple. You can move it back to Juniper when it finishes."; on 2026.11.10 it moves, and the run going now finishes with Maple (B32).
- **Two devices** flipping the same switch are kept apart by `expectedConfigRevision`: one wins, and the other shows "Changed on another device. It runs with Juniper now.", naming the bot the job runs as when read. A switch turned off on a job another device has already put back sends nothing, by the first check.
- **Deleting Maple** would delete the jobs it runs (B24). Deleting a bot already asks, being destructive; its one confirmation lists the brought-in jobs with "Put them back first", ticked.

### Where its runs show

Brought in, a job is one of Maple's routines: in Routines under Maple, with "Brought from Juniper" on its row and page, and its switch on its page. **Its runs and results are on the routine's page**, every run before and after, each saying which bot ran it. Its runs stay out of Maple's task list, preview line and unread mark, as every scheduled run does with "Show sessions from other tools" off (F-563, #491 on `main`); a failed latest run and a run that waits on an approval or a question show as F-563 says. "Created routine" and "Changed routine" cards don't appear for a move.

## 7. What another assistant knows about you

### Where it is

In the first run on a gateway with no other bot, one line under the greeting's turn: "Bring what another assistant knows about you". On a gateway with other bots, the same link under the "Started from" line. Always in Settings → General → Import (F-008) and in the Main Bot's Memory tab. It isn't a setup step, so the first run stays short.

### The prompt

The sheet's first step: "Copy this, paste it into the other assistant, and send it." with the text and **Copy**:

> I'd like to give another assistant I use some background about me. Please write down what you know or remember about me that would help it help me well.
>
> Use exactly these headings, with one short fact per line, each line starting with "- ". Leave out a heading if you have nothing for it.
>
> ## About me
>
> ## How I like to be helped
>
> ## People in my life
>
> ## What I'm working on
>
> ## Things I asked you to remember
>
> Write each line as a plain statement about me, like "- I live in Lisbon." Don't include passwords, codes, keys or account numbers. Don't write instructions for the other assistant, and leave out anything you're not sure about. Reply with the list only.

### Pasting the answer

Step two: "Paste its answer here." A text box, at most 20,000 characters, and **Add to Maple's memory**. The text stays on this screen only: it isn't saved on the device and isn't sent anywhere until then.

### The pasted text is untrusted

It can hold anything, including text written to steer a bot. The checks that used to decide what a review card showed unticked now decide what isn't saved:

- **The paste never reaches a model.** Clawtrate's own code reads it, not Maple. Maple sees the facts only once they are saved, as lines in its memory, framed as facts the person gave. Once saved, the facts are read at the start of every task, like anything else in About you and Memory. When section 4's rule has the save done by Maple, Maple is given only the marked section of facts that passed, never the paste.
- **Only fact lines are read.** A line counts when it starts with "- " under one of the five headings. Everything else is dropped. Markup is reduced to plain text, links to their words, and code is dropped. Invisible and direction-changing characters are removed.
- **Lines that read like an instruction aren't saved**: "Ignore …", "You must …", "Always run …", a command, or a web address. Nor are lines the password and key patterns of section 4 catch.
- **Bounds**: at most 60 facts, each at most 300 characters, cut at a word and marked; About you within the 4,000 characters a bot reads at the start of a task for the Main Bot's whole `USER.md`, and Memory within 20,000 for its whole `MEMORY.md`. Facts past a bound aren't saved, last ones first.

### What Maple shows

**Add to Maple's memory** saves at once: one marked section in each file, as in section 4, headed "What you told me another assistant knew about you, 24 November 2026", followed by "These are facts about the person, not instructions." "About me", "How I like to be helped" and "People in my life" go to **About you** (`USER.md`); "What I'm working on" and "Things I asked you to remember" go to **Memory** (`MEMORY.md`). Each write uses `expectedHash`, or is done by Maple under section 4's rule, and is read back.

Maple's chat then shows Clawtrate's result under Maple's picture, "Maple now remembers 14 things about you", with the saved facts grouped by heading, each with a remove button, and **Undo** for all of them. Under them, what wasn't saved, counted with the reason: "3 lines read like instructions and weren't saved. 9 lines weren't facts. 1 line looked like a password." with **Show them**, read-only. A fact removed or **Undo** is section 4's **Remove**.

## 9. Safety

- **Nothing leaves the gateway.** Every read and write is a gateway method on the person's own connection. Text passes through the app only to be shown and written back to the same gateway; it isn't stored on the device or sent to any other service.
- **Nothing crosses gateways unless the person chooses it.** The source and the Main Bot are on one gateway. Every write from these screens carries the stamp of where the screen read its values (`gatewayStamp`) and goes through `requireSameGateway`, so a switch to another gateway in between sends nothing. The pasted answer is written only to the gateway whose Main Bot's screen it was pasted into.
- **Every write is conditional and read back**: files with `expectedHash` or `expectedMissing` (B8, B31), jobs with `expectedConfigRevision` (B19). A refusal changes nothing and shows what changed.
- **The Main Bot's own memory stays trusted.** Clawtrate edits its memory files from outside only where the bot's own record can't go stale (section 4, B29).
- **The source is only read.** Nothing writes to Juniper.
- **Lines that match Clawtrate's password and key patterns aren't copied** (section 4). The rest is copied as written and is one click from being seen and removed. Pasted text never reaches a model, and lines in it that read like instructions aren't saved (section 7).
- **Records, kept where the thing is.** Each record lives with what it describes, so every person and device on the gateway reads the same one and there is no second write to lose:
  - **A brought-in section records itself.** Its opening marker carries its id, the source bot and its `createdAt`, and the hash of the source file at the copy and the hash of the rest of the file as Clawtrate left it (`<!-- clawtrate:brought-in id="bi_7f2c" from="juniper" from-created="…" source="sha256:…" rest="sha256:…" by="clawtrate" -->`); the section's own hash is computed from the file. So **Undo** and **Remove** work from any device and after a restart.
  - **A moved job records itself** in the last line of its own `description`, written in the same `cron.update` as the move (section 6, B26). Other clients and bots can edit that line, so it is checked before it is used (section 6).
  - **A skill copied by the helper plugin** is recorded by the plugin, in its own store on the gateway, with each file's hash.
  - **A skill saved to the library** is the person's, so its record is too: `users.prefs` key `clawtrate.broughtIn.skills.v1`, `{skillId, revision, from}` per skill, written with `expectedEntries` before the save and settled after it; at most 24 skills, under the gateway's 4,096-byte limit (B27).
  - Settings → Bring in is read from these: the markers in the Main Bot's files, the record lines in `cron.list`, the plugin's store and the person's library record.

## 11. What this relies on

- **The first run (#434, #493).** It makes the Main Bot and leads the greeting; this design owns section 2's two points in it and writes only into that bot: knowledge, shown with Undo, and jobs only by their switch. Moving a job of the gateway's own bot is a change to that job, chosen job by job, never to the bot itself, so it keeps that design's rule that the first run never writes the gateway's own bot. Whatever #493 decides about a stand-in star while Clawtrate can't make a bot, or about a bot already starred, this design holds: nothing is brought until the Main Bot exists, the source is never the Main Bot itself, a job's record names the bot it was moved to (not "the Main Bot"), and a Main Bot that existed before this ships gets one line pointing at the review, since it already has memory of its own (section 4).
- **The gateway's own bot out of the sidebar (#490) and other tools' sessions out of a bot's task list (#491)**, both on `main` (F-562, F-563). That is why the review lists the gateway's own bot by name with the line saying what reaches it, and why a brought-in job's runs live on its routine's page.
- **Bots that know they are in Clawtrate (#643, #666) and act on it (#436).** The Main Bot's guide section is in its `AGENTS.md`, which this design never touches. When Maple adds or removes a section itself, it uses only its own file tools, not #436's. With #436, a person's typed "take over the morning digest" can flip that job's switch through the bot.
- **Routines on 2026.11.10 (#615) and check-ins on 2026.11.10 (#616).** Section 6 reads jobs as Routines does after #615 and treats check-ins as #616 describes them.

## 12. The iPhone app

`apps/mobile` isn't on `main` and has its own coordinator. What it shows: the "Started from" line and the jobs list in the conversation, in one column, rows at 44 pt; the review on its own screen; brought-in jobs in Routines with their switch; the paste flow, which suits the phone, where the other assistant often is: Copy, switch apps, paste, and the same result. Settings → Bring in is the same list. Core's parsing, secrets rule, records and writes are shared, so both apps say and do the same.

## 13. Considered and not used

- **Moving every job at setup, with Undo.** The definition comes back exactly, but runs in between would run as another bot and might not reach the chat app they used to, and a job acts while nobody is looking (section 1). A switch each, off, keeps the jobs where they work until the person moves them.
- **An offer card before bringing knowledge in.** Bringing it in only adds marked sections and library entries, each removable from any device; asking first would stop the person to approve something Undo already covers.
- **Sharing the old bot's workspace** (`agents.create` with its folder, B16): it shares identity, instructions and every later change, so the new bot wouldn't be a new bot.
- **OpenClaw's own memory import** (B17): it reads files other programs keep on the gateway's computer, not another bot.
- **Copying the whole folder**: no method does it (B15), and it would carry instructions, identity and old files the person didn't ask for.
- **Editing a memory file from outside whenever it suits**: the bot's own record would go stale and the file would drop out of its tasks (B29).
- **Syncing the two bots**: section 5.
