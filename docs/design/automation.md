# Routines and check-ins

How a person sees the gateway's scheduled jobs (routines) across bots and for one bot, reads a routine and its runs, follows them live, creates and changes them with admin access, and sets a bot's proactive check-ins. This part of the design comes after `visual-language.md`, `conversation.md`, `work-pane.md` and `bots.md`, in the same language and with the same tokens.

What must be true is in `docs/parity.md`, the 28 rows of the routines and check-ins section (tracking issue #1; rows listed in section 17). What the gateway can do is in `docs/research/gateway-capabilities.md`, row 9 ("Schedules") and row 21 ("Routines"; "Proactive check-ins"). This document decides appearance, wording and input, and for every state it names the gateway fact it rests on. Section 16 lists those facts with their sources and says which were confirmed on a running development gateway for this design and which were only read. Section 17 lists the parity rows whose wording changes. Section 18 is for builders.

The rule from `conversation.md` holds here: **no state claims more than the gateway establishes.** Three facts shape everything below. Each says how far it was observed on a development gateway and where it rests on reading alone (section 16 has the rows):

- A routine has no webhook of its own and no run-limit setting. The webhook's absence is read from the schema (row 19); hooks were not exercised. For the limit, `cron.update` refused `maxRunsPerHour` as an unknown field (row 21, observed): that shows no such setting can be written, not that the gateway never limits runs internally, which was neither documented nor reached. The routine page says what triggers it, and nothing in the editor offers either.
- A routine's run is not a task the gateway lists: `chat.history` on an isolated run's session returned nothing (observed); that `sessions.list` leaves it out is from the capability map (row 9). Its conversation is read through the routine (`cron.history`, observed), and only when that returns it; the gateway's 24-hour pruning of run sessions is read from its documentation, not observed (row 12).
- Check-ins are one gateway setting per bot with a catch: once any bot has its own setting, only bots with their own setting check in (row 25, observed). Turning one bot's check-ins on can silently turn another's off unless Clawtrate writes both.

The prototype shows all of it: `prototype/routines.html`, reachable from `prototype/index.html` and `prototype/gallery.html`. Its address picks any state (section 18). The styles are in `prototype/css/routines.css`, the behaviour in `prototype/js/routines.js`, and `prototype/tools/checks/routines.mjs` checks every state's words and structure.

## 1. Where routines live

```
rail top, "Routines" (clock icon)    the Routines view in the main pane, across bots
  List | Day | 3 days | Week          the list (section 3) or the calendar (section 4)
  "All bots" select, or one bot       a bot's own routines, with its check-ins on top
  a row                               the routine's page (section 6)
    Runs                              newest first; a run opens its conversation (section 7)
  "New routine" (admin)               the editor (section 9)
rail, under a bot                     a routine's latest run, as a task row with a clock (section 7)
conversation                          "Created routine" and "Changed routine" cards (conversation.md §8.2)
                                      the "Checked in" line over a check-in's reply (section 13)
```

Grok Bot keeps routines in a bot's profile and reaches them from chat. Clawtrate keeps that route (a bot's own routines are one choice away, and the cards in the conversation open a routine) and adds what Grok Bot lacks: one place for every routine on the gateway, a calendar, run history with each run's conversation, and a form.

The Routines view takes the conversation's place in the main pane, as Settings' sections take a dialog's: the rail stays, the Work pane closes, and Back (or choosing a task in the rail) returns to the conversation. It is a view of its own, not a tab of the Work pane, for two reasons: routines belong to the gateway, not to one task, and the Work pane and a bot's settings are being changed by the maintainers (tracking issue, "Not yours for now"). Section 18 lists the three small links into those areas that this design asks for, each to be agreed in an issue first.

### Access

Reading routines, their runs and their conversations needs `operator.read` (section 16, rows 1, 6). Every change needs `operator.admin`: create, edit, pause and resume, run now, delete, and a bot's check-ins (rows 1, 17). There is no lower path; the gateway refuses `cron.add` on a read connection with "missing scope: operator.admin" (row 1).

So a read-only connection sees everything and changes nothing, and the request for admin access is the access bar of `bots.md` §2, with its states (asking, requested, denied, expired, couldn't ask, granted), at the foot of the view. Its words here: "**Read-only.** Creating and changing routines needs admin access." (read tier); "**Admin access needed.** Creating and changing routines needs admin access." (interactive tier). Admin is asked for only by that button, never on opening the view and never by an action that would otherwise do nothing (section 11).

### Input

- The view is reached with the rail's "Routines" button. The view switch is a radio group with one tab stop: arrows move and choose; Home and End go to the ends.
- A routine row is one tab stop; Enter or Space opens its page; the context-menu key, Shift+F10 or a right-click opens its menu (section 10). Its switch is a separate tab stop.
- In the calendar, Tab moves through chips day by day in time order; Left and Right move to the chip closest in time on the nearest earlier or later day that has one (at the range's edge they do nothing; Previous and Next move the range); Enter opens the routine.
- Dialogs follow `bots.md` §2: modal, Tab stays inside, Escape closes, focus returns to the control that opened it. While a dialog's Save is in flight, Escape waits for the answer (section 9).
- One polite live region announces: "Routine created", "Saved", "Paused <name>", "Resumed <name>", "Running <name>", "<name> finished", "<name> failed", "Deleted <name>", "<name> was changed elsewhere", "<name> was deleted elsewhere", "Check-ins saved", and the access bar's phrases. Events from other clients are announced only for the routine on screen and for failures (section 8).

## 4. The Routines view: the calendar

Day, 3 days and Week (F-197) draw the same routines as occurrences in time. The days start today; "Previous" and "Next" (round buttons, chevrons, named "Previous 7 days" and "Next 7 days", or "day" and "3 days") move the dates by the view's length, and "Today" returns. The range is said beside them, "Today Nov 18 to Tue Nov 24", as a status line, so moving is announced; focus stays on the button pressed. The hour scale is the viewer's own time zone, said once in the corner ("Your time, New York"), because routines in different zones must line up on one scale.

- Occurrences are computed by Clawtrate from each routine's schedule and time zone, from now on (the gateway gives only the next one; row 7). Before now, the calendar draws runs: the entries `cron.runs` returned (the 20 most recent per routine), of every routine, paused, frequent or not, each with how it ended. So the past is what happened and the future is what is scheduled, and the rules below for what is drawn ahead never hide a run that happened. Within a day, chips are in time order.
- Occurrences of a cron schedule are its firing times under the cron rule of section 9 (croner's grammar), in the schedule's `tz`, with daylight-saving transitions as described there.
- A routine that occurs more than four times a day (an interval under 6 hours, or a cron expression with more than four firing times in a day) is not drawn ahead as chips; it gets one band at the top of each day from today on: "Disk space watch · every 30 minutes", or the expression as written ("Often · */5 * * * *"). Its past runs are chips like any other. A routine with a condition script is drawn by its schedule, and the band's tooltip adds "only when its check passes".
- A paused routine is not drawn ahead; its past runs stay. Check-ins are not drawn: they are one line under the day heads, "Check-ins: Juniper every 30 minutes", because a check-in that finds nothing says nothing (section 13).
- A schedule Clawtrate cannot compute (an `on-exit` or `stream` trigger, `pacing`, a cron schedule without `tz` whose gateway host zone is unknown, or a cron expression this client's reader refuses though the gateway saved it) is drawn only at the gateway's own next run, `state.nextRunAtMs`, with "and later, as the gateway decides" in its tooltip; with no `nextRunAtMs` it is not drawn ahead. Its row's "Next" uses the same value.
- Chips never cover each other. Chips that start less than an hour apart on one day (a chip, or its 44 px hit area on touch, is under an hour tall) share the row side by side in time order, each with an equal share of the day's width and its own target, so each opens by pointer and by keyboard.
- A top-of-the-hour cron schedule may start up to 5 minutes late, which the gateway does on purpose to spread load (row 8); chips sit at the written time and the tooltip says "may start up to 5 minutes later".

The week is seven columns at 1440 and 1100, four at 800 with horizontal scrolling inside the calendar only, and on the phone the view switch offers List and Day only.

## 7. A run

A run is opened from its row on the routine's page, or from the rail. What it can show depends on the routine and on what the gateway returns for that run:

- **An isolated agent turn** (`sessionTarget: "isolated"`, `payload.kind: "agentTurn"`). The gateway gives each run a session of its own, `agent:<bot>:cron:<job>:run:<session>`, which `chat.history` reads as empty (row 9). Clawtrate asks `cron.history {id, runId}` (read tier). Only when that returns messages for this run is there a transcript; that is the positive evidence, and nothing else (the run's age, its place in the list, the retention setting) stands in for it. An empty `messages` and the refusal "The recorded cron transcript is unavailable. Refresh the run and try again." (row 29) both mean no transcript.
- **A run in a shared conversation** (`main`, `current` or a session key), which includes every reminder (`systemEvent`). Its turn is in that conversation among the others. `cron.history` gives it no transcript of its own: for a reminder's forced run it answered "The recorded cron transcript is unavailable. Refresh the run and try again." (row 29), and the gateway's documentation says a shared session's history spans its runs. Clawtrate does not ask, and does not cut a piece out of that conversation.
- **A command** (`payload.kind: "command"`) runs on the gateway, not a bot, and has no conversation.

In every case the run's `cron.runs` entry is known: its outcome, `summary`, duration and usage.

**Which run is which (row 30).** A run is identified by its job's id and its `runId`, which `finished`, `cron.run` and `cron.runs` entries carry when the gateway has one; `runId` is optional in the pinned schema, on the event and on a `cron.runs` entry alike. A run in progress has no `runId` yet: `started` gives only `runAtMs`, which `job.state.runningAtMs` repeats. Until its own end it is identified by its job's id and that `runAtMs`; its end is the `finished` event for the same job with the same `runAtMs`, and from then on it is identified by the `runId` that event carries. A `finished` with any other `runAtMs` is another run: it is placed among the runs by `runAtMs`, newest first, and the running row stays. A `finished` or an entry that has no `runId` is identified by its job's id and `runAtMs`, and its transcript is asked for as `cron.history {id, runAtMs}`, which the pinned schema accepts in place of `runId`. The `finished` event's fields are at its top level, as section 8 lists them (`jobId`, `runId`, `runAtMs`, `status`, …), not in a nested run object. The open run's view, its row's focus on return, and `cron.history` all use this identity, never a position in the list, so a run that arrives while one is being read never replaces it.

**In the rail (F-193).** Under its bot, each isolated agent routine whose latest run finished in the last 24 hours (Clawtrate's choice, the gateway's default retention) appears once, as a task row: the clock icon in place of the title's start, the routine's name as the title, the latest run's time, and its outcome as the preview ("Succeeded", "Failed: …"), with the failed mark when it failed. It takes part in the bot's six-row cap and its ordering like any task. Opening it opens that run, which then shows what the gateway returns for it, as below. It never offers "New task", rename, pin or delete; its menu has "Open routine" only.

**The run's view.** The conversation column, with:

- Header: one way back, "Back to Morning digest, all runs", to the routine's page and its runs; the routine's name as the title (shortened at the end, the full name in its tooltip); under it the bot and the run's time, "Marlow · Today 8:00 AM", so a long name never cuts the time off. There is no second "All runs" control: it would lead to the same place.
- First, the scheduled message as an event line card: the clock, "Scheduled message, as the gateway recorded it", and the history's first message in full, line breaks kept. That message is the job's instruction as it read when the run started, followed by the scheduler's own instructions for unattended runs, and the history marks it as the scheduler's (`provenance.kind: "internal_system"`, `sourceTool: "cron"`, row 10). Clawtrate does not split it: the routine's instruction may have been edited since, so the current instruction is never used to find where the recorded one ends, and never stands in for it.
- Then the bot's work and reply, as any turn (`conversation.md`): cards, steps and the bubble.
- In place of the composer, a quiet foot: "This run has finished. A run can't be answered; to change what it does, edit the routine." with "Edit routine" (admin) and the run's facts in one line: "scripted/dev-1 · 2.6 s · 120 in, 80 out tokens".

New agent turns always run in a new conversation each time. Edits keep the existing session target, including reminders in the main conversation. Clawtrate writes `cron.add {name, agentId, schedule, sessionTarget: "isolated", wakeMode: "now", payload: {kind: "agentTurn", message}, delivery}` and `cron.update {id, patch}` with only the fields that differ from what the editor opened with. The daily kinds become `{kind: "cron", expr, tz}` ("0 8 * * 1-5" for weekdays at 8:00); the repeating kind `{kind: "every", everyMs}`; Once `{kind: "at", at}`, where `at` is a string: the chosen date and time in the chosen zone, converted to that instant and written as ISO 8601 with the zone's offset at that instant ("2026-11-20T10:00:00+01:00" for 10:00 AM in Paris). **Delivery is an object (rows 13, 30).** The pinned schema takes `delivery` only as an object, on `cron.add` and in a `cron.update` patch; the gateway refused the string `"none"` ("invalid cron.add params: at /delivery: must be object"). Results "Keep them with the run" writes `{mode: "none"}` and "Also announce to the gateway's channels" writes `{mode: "announce"}`. `cron.add` always sends `delivery`, defaulting to `{mode: "none"}`, because a development or channel-less gateway otherwise fails the run's delivery after the work succeeded. An edit sends `patch.delivery` only when Results changed, and then only `{mode}`: the gateway merges a delivery patch into the job's delivery field by field, so `channel`, `to`, `threadId`, `accountId`, `bestEffort` and `failureDestination` are kept (observed: a job with `{mode: "none", failureDestination: {mode: "announce", channel: "last"}}` patched with `{mode: "announce"}` read back as `{mode: "announce", failureDestination: {mode: "announce", channel: "last"}}`). A job read with no `delivery` is shown with "Also announce" chosen, the gateway's default for an isolated agent turn. "Save as a new routine" sends the deleted job's whole `delivery` object when Results was not changed. Every request is valid against `CronAddParams` or `CronUpdateParams` in the pinned `protocol.schema.json`, and the checks validate each one.

**Validation, before any request.**

- The cron field is read with the gateway's own parser: croner 10.0.1, the version OpenClaw 2026.10.6 depends on, constructed as the gateway does, `new Cron(expr, {timezone: tz, catch: false})` (row 30). That grammar is five fields, six (seconds first) or seven (then the year); names (`JAN`, `MON`), `*`, `?`, ranges, `*/n` and `a-b/n` steps (not `5/10`), lists, `L`, `nW` and `LW` in the day of the month, `n#k`, `n#L` and `nL` in the day of the week, a leading `+` there for "day of the month and day of the week" (otherwise either one), and `@yearly`, `@annually`, `@monthly`, `@weekly`, `@daily`, `@midnight`, `@hourly`. An expression croner refuses gets "The gateway can't read this: <the reason>." under the field, and is not sent.
- **The 5-minute floor (F-195).** A routine may not fire twice within 5 minutes. For the repeating kind: an interval below 5 minutes. For a cron expression, three builders must get the same answer, so the rule is exact:
  - **Grammar and days.** What is accepted, and which days fire, are exactly croner 10.0.1's as the gateway constructs it, `new Cron(expr, {timezone: tz, catch: false})`, with nothing added or taken away. Two consequences that differ from other cron readers: `?` lists every value but is not `*`, so `0,59 0,23 1 * ?` fires on the 1st or on any day of the week, which is every day; and a range has exactly two ends, so `0 0-8-9 * * *` is refused ("illegal range").
  - **The window.** 2,922 calendar days (eight years), starting with today's date on the schedule's own clock (its `tz`, or the gateway's zone when it has none), today included whole, its times already past included: the spacing is a property of the pattern, not of the moment it is checked.
  - **The spacing.** Take the firing times of one day, in seconds after midnight on the schedule's nominal wall clock (every hour × minute × second it lists, sorted). The spacing is the smallest of (a) the difference between two consecutive firing times in that list, and (b) for every two consecutive dates in the window that both fire, the first firing time plus 86,400 minus the last. Below 300 seconds: "Routines run at least 5 minutes apart." under the field. So `*/7 * * * *` fails (56 to the next hour's 0 is 4 minutes), as do `1-59/7 * * * *`, `*/5,*/7 * * * *`, `0,59 23,0 * * *` (23:59 to 0:00), `0,59 0,23 L,1 * *` (the last day to the 1st) and `0,59 0,23 1 * ?` (23:59 to 0:00, every day); `*/5 * * * *`, `0-1/4 * * * *` (once an hour) and `0,59 8 * * *` pass.
  - **Never.** An expression with no firing at or after now in the window gets "This never runs in the next eight years." The preview's next runs are read in the same window, so the preview and the check never disagree about whether it runs.
  - **Daylight saving.** The floor is a rule on the schedule's nominal wall clock. It does not guarantee five minutes between actual firings across a daylight-saving change, and Clawtrate does not claim it does. OpenClaw's own next-run function (`computeNextRunAtMs`, row 30) fires `0 * * * *` in New York at 06:00Z, 07:00Z and 08:00Z on 2027-03-14, so an hourly schedule stays an hour apart; but it fires `0 0,59 1,3 * * *` at 06:59Z and 07:00Z that day, one real minute apart, though on the wall clock 1:59 to 3:00 is an hour. Measuring real time instead would refuse or accept a schedule depending on which day it was checked. Occurrence previews and the calendar follow the gateway's transition behavior: it runs only at an instant whose wall time matches the pattern (`cron.match`), so a wall time the clocks skip does not run that day. Croner alone shifts it forward by the size of the gap, and the gateway skips that candidate. In New York, `30 2 * * *` runs at 2:30 AM on Mar 13, 2027, then on Mar 15. The floor still uses the nominal wall time. The calendar and preview list distinct instants in ascending order. For `0 0,30 2,3 14 3 *` in New York, successive gateway next-run calculations give 07:00Z and 07:30Z on Mar 14, 2027, then the first run in 2028. Croner's bulk iterator repeats the two 2027 instants.

  This is Clawtrate's floor; the gateway accepts a one-second interval (row 14). Save stays enabled; pressing it focuses the first error.

- "Choose at least one day." for "Some days of the week" with none chosen.
- "Choose a time." for the daily kinds, and "Choose a date and a time." for Once, when a field is empty or not a valid value. These are checked before anything builds the schedule, and the preview then reads "Choose a date and a time to see when it runs." (or "Choose a time …") in place of the next runs.
- "Pick a time in the future." for Once in the past.
- For Once, a date and time the clocks skip in the chosen zone, such as 2:30 AM on Mar 14, 2027 in New York, has no instant, and is refused under When, never moved to another time: "2:30 AM doesn't happen on Mar 14, 2027 in New York: the clocks go forward past it. Pick another time." The preview says the same in place of the next runs.

These are checked only for a new routine or a schedule the person changed: a saved schedule left alone is never refused for what it already is.

## 11. Without admin access

Everything is readable on a read or interactive connection: the list, the calendar, pages, runs and their conversations, check-ins. What changes:

- No switches. "Paused" and the state line carry the state.
- No "New routine", no Run now, Pause, Edit or Delete, and a row's menu has "Open" only. Nothing is shown disabled: an action that would do nothing is not offered (`bots.md` §2).
- The access bar at the foot of the view, with this view's words (section 2). After "Admin access granted", the controls appear where they belong and focus stays where it was.
- A bot's check-ins block shows its values and no "Change".
- The empty state says "No routines yet" and its sentence, with no button.
- A link or an address that would open the editor, the delete confirmation or the check-ins setting opens only the view behind it: no dialog, no switch, no Save. The tier is checked where the action starts, not only where its button is drawn, so every way in meets the same guard.

There is no write-tier path to pause or run a routine. A plugin could offer one (row 18, an unverified proposal in the capability map); this design does not assume it.

## 13. Check-ins

A bot's check-ins are the gateway's heartbeat: on a cadence, the gateway runs a turn in the bot's main conversation with a prompt. The gateway's built-in prompt asks the bot to answer only when something needs the person and otherwise to reply `NO_REPLY`, and a `NO_REPLY` reply is not shown or kept (rows 25-28). Staying quiet is what that prompt requests of the bot, not something the gateway enforces, so no words here promise it. The gateway also takes a custom prompt per bot and a custom default for every bot, so an empty per-bot prompt does not by itself mean the built-in one.

**Where.** At the top of the Routines view when one bot is chosen, in a block of its own, "Check-ins", above that bot's routines. In the list across bots, each group head says the bot's check-ins at its right: "Checks in every 30 minutes", "Checks in daily", "No check-ins".

**What it reads.** The gateway keeps one job per bot that checks in, in `cron.list` (read tier), with `payload.kind: "heartbeat"`, its cadence in `schedule.everyMs`, `enabled`, and `state.nextRunAtMs`. That job is what will actually run, so it is the source for the block's words. The prompt and the per-bot setting are in the gateway's config, read with admin.

```
Check-ins                                                        [Change]
Every 30 minutes, Juniper looks for anything that needs you.
Its prompt asks it to stay quiet when nothing does.
Next check-in 9:30 AM · In Juniper's main conversation
```

The words after the cadence are said from what Clawtrate knows of the prompt in effect, never from reading a prompt's wording for intent:

- **The built-in prompt**, known only when the per-bot prompt is empty and the gateway's default (`agents.defaults.heartbeat.prompt`) is unset, both read with admin: "<Cadence>, Juniper looks for anything that needs you. Its prompt asks it to stay quiet when nothing does."
- **A prompt of the bot's own**, whatever it says, `NO_REPLY` mentioned or not: "<Cadence>, Juniper checks in with a prompt of its own."
- **An inherited custom default**: "<Cadence>, Juniper checks in with the prompt the gateway sets for every bot."
- **A prompt Clawtrate can't read** (below admin, or the config read failed): "<Cadence>, Juniper checks in. Clawtrate can't read its prompt."

<Cadence> is "Every 30 minutes", "Once a day", or the interval in words; in the setting below, Every… says "As often as you choose". The same sentence is under Daily, As needed and Every… in the setting, and follows the prompt field as it is typed.

**The setting (F-201).** "Change" (admin) opens a dialog, "Juniper's check-ins", with a choice list:

| Choice    | Words under it                                              | Writes `agents.entries.<bot>.heartbeat` |
| --------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Off       | "Juniper doesn't check in on a schedule."                   | `every: "0m"`                           |
| Daily     | The prompt-specific sentence above, with "Once a day"       | `every: "24h"`                          |
| As needed | The prompt-specific sentence above, with "Every 30 minutes" | `every: "30m"`                          |
| Every…    | A select: 15 minutes, 1, 2, 4, 8 or 12 hours                | `every` as chosen                       |

"As needed" cannot mean that the bot decides when to look: the gateway has no such mode. It means frequent checks; with the built-in prompt, those checks ask the bot to stay silent unless something needs the person (row 26). The words say that it is asked, not that it happens, and say it only for the built-in prompt.

Under the choices, "What to check" (a disclosure, closed): the prompt. Empty means the gateway's default; when that is unset it is the built-in prompt, shown as faint text: "Follow the heartbeat monitor scratch context when provided. … If nothing needs attention, reply NO_REPLY." (the documented default of 2026.10.6). When the gateway's default is a custom prompt, the field's hint says "Empty uses the prompt the gateway sets for every bot." and no built-in text is shown. With the built-in default, a typed prompt gets this note: "This prompt replaces the gateway's own, which asks Juniper to reply NO_REPLY when nothing needs attention." With a custom or unreadable default, the note says "This prompt replaces the gateway's default for this bot." Both end with "Whether Juniper stays quiet depends on what this prompt asks." One scripted bot answered a custom prompt (row 27), which shows that a check-in can speak, not what any given prompt will do. Save writes one `config.patch`, then reads `cron.list` until the bot's check-in job shows the new cadence, and only then says "Check-ins saved". While it saves, the dialog stays open and Escape waits, as in the editor (section 9).

**The catch (row 25).** When no bot has a setting of its own, the gateway's default decides, and on a gateway with several bots only one bot checks in: the one named as the default's owner (on the development gateway, Juniper, every 30 minutes). As soon as any bot has a setting of its own, only the bots with their own setting check in. So saving a setting for one bot while another checks in through the default would stop the other. Clawtrate writes both in the same patch: the bot being changed, and every bot whose check-in job exists now, with its current cadence. The dialog says so before saving, as a neutral note: "Juniper checks in now through the gateway's default. Saving gives Juniper a setting of its own with the same cadence, so it keeps checking in." Observed: writing Marlow's setting removed Juniper's check-in job; removing Marlow's setting brought it back.

Before confirming a save, read back the changed cadence and prompt fields from the gateway configuration. Use the heartbeat job's `enabled` and schedule to confirm what will run.

**Off, and what Off does not stop (F-366).** Off writes `every: "0m"`: the bot's check-in job stays, disabled, and no scheduled check-in runs (row 25, observed). The gateway still starts a turn when a command the bot ran in the background finishes, to report it; that is the bot finishing its own work, not a check-in, and the setting says so under Off: "Juniper still reports when a command it started in the background finishes." (row 28, read only.)

**Telling a check-in apart.**

The check-in labels rest on one piece of positive evidence: the run's `agent` events carried `isHeartbeat: true` while Clawtrate was connected (row 27, observed). Clawtrate keeps those run ids, bounded, while their task is open. Nothing else counts: not the absence of a person's message before a reply, not the hour, not the wording.

- In the transcript: a reply whose run was identified that way gets an event line above its bubble, the clock and "Checked in · 9:30 AM". A reply read from history without that record is drawn as an ordinary reply, with no line and no guess at why the bot wrote it: in history, a check-in's reply, a finished background command's report and other turns the gateway starts can look alike.
- In the rail: the task's preview starts "Checked in: " only while the latest activity is a reply identified that way; otherwise it is the reply's text as for any task. A silent check-in changes nothing anywhere: no preview, no time, no unread dot.
- A check-in that is running shows the working mark like any run, with "Checking in" on line two.

**Time zone (F-196).** Under the check-ins block, one fact: "Routines' time zone: New York", or "Mixed: New York, Paris" when they differ. "Change…" (admin) opens a dialog, "Pip's routines' time zone": the zone select ("Auto-detect (New York)", then the zone list), then "Changes these 2 routines:" with each routine's name and its zone now ("Plan the week (now London)"), then "Left alone:" with each routine that has no zone or a schedule the form does not write, and why ("it has no time zone", "its schedule was set on the gateway"). "Change" (primary) becomes "Changing…" and rewrites each listed routine's `schedule` with the new `tz` through `cron.update`, one by one. If the set of routines changes before the writes, show the updated list and ask the person to confirm again. When all succeed, the dialog closes, "Changed the time zone of 2 routines" is announced, and focus returns to "Change…". When the gateway refuses a write, the dialog stays with a note naming what changed and what the gateway refused, and focus moves to "Close". An unanswered write has an unknown outcome. Read each affected routine back with `cron.get {id}` and compare its schedule with the one sent. A match counts as changed. If the schedule differs, say what the gateway now lists and that the change may still arrive. If a read fails, name the unconfirmed routines, say "Routines' time zone: checking needed" on the zone line and focus "Check now", which retries only the unresolved reads. While a read is still needed, keep the dialog open and offer no second write. Once the reads confirm what the gateway lists, show the zones it reported.

**Offers (F-200).** A bot that spots work it could take on says so in its own reply, in an ordinary task or as a check-in's reply. Clawtrate draws that reply as any other: no offer card, no Accept button, because the gateway marks nothing in a reply as an offer. The person takes it up by answering, and the work that follows is an ordinary run, which asks before risky actions as any run does (F-365). The row's check uses a development-gateway fixture that gives the bot a pending item, and asserts the reply and the run that follows, not a control of Clawtrate's.

**A connector that stopped working (F-364).** When a bot's tool for an app such as Gmail fails, the failure shows where any tool failure does: the action card in its failed state with the tool's own error text, and its Raw call one step further (`conversation.md`). What the bot then says, including any offer to reconnect, is its reply. Clawtrate adds no "Signed out" badge and no Reconnect button of its own: the gateway reports no connector sign-in state (F-218), so a failed or missing tool is what Clawtrate knows, and a sign-out is said only in the tool's or the bot's words.

**When the scheduler is off** (`cron.status` `enabled: false`), the block says "The gateway's scheduler is off, so scheduled check-ins don't run." (row 3, observed on the development gateway).

## 14. Away for a while

The gateway does not notice that a person has stopped using it (F-194, row 23). Clawtrate keeps the time it was last opened by this person in `users.prefs`, so desktop and phone agree. When it is opened after 14 days or more (Clawtrate's choice) and routines are enabled, the Routines button gets a dot ("Routines, needs your answer") and the view opens with a card at the top:

> **You've been away for a bit.** 5 routines kept running while you were away. Keep them running?
> [Keep them running] [Pause them all] [Keep running, don't ask again]
> If you don't answer, Clawtrate pauses them on Nov 25, the first time it is open with admin access on or after that day.

- "Pause them all" pauses every enabled routine with `cron.update` one by one and lists any that failed. Below admin it is absent and the card says "Pausing them needs admin access."; the view's access bar is the way to ask.
- F-357: the 7-day pause is Clawtrate's; it happens only when Clawtrate is open with admin access on or after that day, which the card's last line says. A gateway that no Clawtrate opens keeps running its routines.
- "Don't ask again" is stored with the same preference.
- The card's dates and answers are written with `users.prefs.set`, which needs `operator.write`. On a read-only connection Clawtrate can read when it was last opened but can keep no answer, so it shows no card and no dot: a question whose answer would be forgotten is not asked, and that connection could not pause anything either.
- When `users.prefs` answers `no_durable_identity` (a shared-token connection has no per-person store), Clawtrate keeps the last-opened time, the posted date and the answer on this device only, and the card adds "This gateway can't keep your answer for your other devices, so Clawtrate keeps it on this one; another device may ask again."
