# Group chats

How a person talks to two to six bots in one place: making a group, how it shows in the list, who answers a message, what happens while several bots work, approvals, and where the group is kept. This part follows `visual-language.md`, `conversation.md`, `bots.md`, `work-pane.md`, `controls.md` and `setup.md`, in the same language and with the same tokens.

What must be true is in `docs/parity.md`, rows F-029, F-077 to F-080, F-179 and F-288 to F-290. What the gateway does is in `docs/research/group-chats.md`; every state below names the fact it rests on, and section 14 lists them. Section 16 is for builders.

The gateway has no conversation shared by several bots (research §1). So one rule sits above the rest: **a group is one task per bot, shown together, and the screen never says the bots are in one conversation.** Clawtrate sends each bot a separate copy and passes no bot's reply to another, and the group says so where a person starts writing. Everything a group shows is something one of its tasks reported; everything it sends is an ordinary message to one bot's task.

The prototype shows all of it: `prototype/groups.html` is the app with groups added, and its address picks any state (section 16). The styles are in `prototype/css/groups.css` and the behaviour in `prototype/js/groups.js`. `prototype/tools/checks/groups.mjs` checks it.

## 1. How the pieces fit

```
rail                     bots and groups, several groups among them, in one list by recent activity (F-029)
  group row                combined avatar, name, time; "Otto: Done. The old cache…" (section 5)
  bot row                  as conversation.md §20; a member task sits in its bot's task list too
"+" menu                 New task, New bot, New group (section 4)
group conversation       one column, every member's task merged by the gateway's time (section 6)
  header                   the title pill: combined avatar and the group's name; opens Group details
  first line               "Clawtrate sends each bot a separate copy…" (section 3)
  your message             once, with one line saying where it went (section 6)
  each reply               the bot's name over it, its avatar at the foot (F-078)
  status lines             one per member that is working, in member order (section 8)
  composer                 the To line, @ mentions, Reply and Quote (section 7); Stop asks the gateway to stop eligible runs (section 8)
Group details            name, members and their tasks, add, remove, remove the group (section 10)
```

A group is a record kept with the person's preferences on the gateway (section 10), naming two to six tasks, one per bot. The tasks are ordinary tasks: each is also in its own bot's task list, and opening it there shows that bot's side alone, which is exactly what that bot has seen.

### Tokens

None added. The group uses `--bubble-user` in its neutral form for your bubbles (section 6) and the bot characters' own colours only inside their pictures.

### Access

Reading a group needs only read: the record is read with `users.prefs.get` and every member's task with `chat.history` and `sessions.messages.subscribe` (`operator.read`, section 14). Making, renaming, changing or removing a group, and sending in one, need the interactive tier (`operator.write`), requested as `conversation.md` §3 requests it, never silently. Restoring an archived member task is `sessions.patch` at write. Nothing in this design needs admin.

### Announcements

One polite live region: "Making the group", "Made the group", "Couldn't make Marlow's task", "Saved the name", "Removed Marlow from the group", "Removed the group", "Sent to Juniper, Otto and Marlow", "Stopping 2 bots", "Stopped", "Couldn't stop Otto's run from this device", "No answer about Otto's run", "Using Marlow's new task", and the conversation's own lifecycle phrases with the bot's name ("Otto is working", "Otto finished").

### Input

Dialogs follow `bots.md` §2: modal, Escape closes, focus returns to what opened them. ⌘⇧N opens New group where "+" offers it. In a group's composer `@` opens the mention list; Up and Down move, Enter inserts, Escape closes it and leaves the `@` typed. The group's composer has no Attach control: attachments to a group are not designed here (section 17).

## 3. What a group is, said on the screen

The gateway keeps no shared conversation (research §1), and Clawtrate does not pass replies from one bot to another: doing so would send messages in the person's name that the person did not write, make every reply a turn for every other bot, and need a bound that only this one device would know (research §4). A bot can still message another bot with its own messaging tool, under its messaging settings (`controls.md` §11); that is the bot's act, shown as its Messaged card, and it goes where the bot points it, usually not into this group (research §4). So the claim is about what Clawtrate does, said where the person writes:

- **The first line of every group**, above its first message, 13 px `--text-muted`: "Clawtrate sends each bot a separate copy. It doesn't pass bot replies between tasks."
- **The empty group** says the same under its name, then "Write to everyone, or start with @ to choose who answers."
- **Group details** says under Members: "Each member has its own task with you. Opening it shows your messages to that bot and its replies."

A person who wants one bot to read another's answer quotes it (section 7). What the bot then receives is the person's own message, as written.

## 4. New group

Below the list: the name the group will have, "Name: Robin, Juniper and Otto", built from the person's own name from `users.self` (`operator.read`) and the chosen bots in the order chosen, and "You can rename it later." With no display name on the profile it starts with "You". Then the note, `.b-note` neutral: "Clawtrate makes a new task with each bot and keeps the group with your preferences on the gateway, so your other devices show it."

**What "made" means.** Each member's task is one `sessions.create {agentId}` (`operator.write`), made without a label, because a label must be unique per bot and a second group with the same name and bot would be refused (research §3). Then the record is written with `users.prefs.set`, as "must be absent" (`expectedEntries: {key: null}`), and read back with `users.prefs.get`. The group is shown only when the read-back holds it. A task made is announced only when its `sessions.create` returned a key.

**When a step fails**, the dialog stays and says exactly what exists. The record write follows section 10's rule with expected absence: compare the read-back with the requested record after every outcome; Check again only reads; Try again takes a new pre-read and uses the tasks already made.

## 6. The group's conversation

**The header.** The title pill holds the combined avatar at 22 px and the group's name; it opens Group details (section 10). There is no Work pane for a group: the pane belongs to one bot's task, and each member's is reached from its task.

Show the newest available history page for each current member task, up to 200 rows per task. Where any response has `hasMore`, say "Showing recent history for Juniper, Otto and Marlow" with the member names in view. Saved message order is the gateway timestamp, breaking ties by current member order and then each task's transcript position. That tie-break is chosen by Clawtrate. A live reply sits at the foot and takes its saved position when it is read back. Keep the same visible text at the reading line through the insertion, using the task transcript's anchor. Use the history request and the recorded assignment to name the bot. Join only saved user copies whose key belongs to this group and member task. Name only the current member histories that actually contain a copy. Render tool and reasoning items through the existing task mapping, and load pending cards through the task's access paths. Reuse the task's tool payload sources for Raw. A messages-only subscription provides item summaries for a run from another device. (F-078)

Apply history, receipts, runs and card events to the gateway, group and task identity that originated them. The open column renders only its current member tasks and its own local outbox. Switching groups keeps both columns' pending work with its original tasks.

**Your bubbles** are right-aligned in the neutral form of `--bubble-user` (`--bg-inverse` with `--text-inverse`), the same as the "Your messages: neutral" choice: a group has no one bot's colour (F-078). A message not yet sent still uses the raised fill (`conversation.md` §6).

**Where it went** (`.g-went`), under your bubble, says each target's state in words. On the device that sent it, every word rests on that copy's own answer or events; `started` from `chat.send` is an admission, not a delivery (research §3):

Copies in the same state are joined: "Sent to Juniper, Otto and Marlow"; "Sent to Juniper and Otto · Couldn't send to Marlow". The line is a button that opens a short list, one row per target with its state and, for a refusal, the gateway's words. It is part of the bubble's accessible name: "You said: Plan Saturday. Sent to Juniper, Otto and Marlow." A message is called "Sent" without qualification only when every copy is Sent or Steer.

**On a device that did not send it**, or after the sending device reloads, only the task histories are known: the line reads "In Juniper's, Otto's and Marlow's task histories", naming the tasks whose histories hold a copy with that send's key. Refusals and copies held or queued on the sending device are not in any history, so they stay on the sending device only, and nothing is said of them elsewhere. Copies in a removed member's history remain in that member's task and leave the group column.

**Each reply.** Bot bubbles are left-aligned as in a task, with the speaker line over the first bubble of each run of one bot's bubbles and the bot's avatar at the foot of the last (F-078). The speaker's name is in `--text-muted`, not in the bot's colour: every character's colour measures under 4.5:1 as 12 px text on the light stage (section 15). Action cards, Messaged cards, "Thought" lines and run lines belong to that bot's run and sit under its speaker line exactly as `conversation.md` §7 to §11 draw them. A Messaged card shows where the message went, which is wherever the bot sent it, usually that bot's main task, not the group (research §4).

Group history, previews, bot pictures, reply and quote text, Raw and cards follow [Addresses a gateway hands the client](../decisions.md#addresses-a-gateway-hands-the-client). A group adds no fetch or open action. Render names, quoted text and gateway reasons as text. An inherited address action uses the task's existing policy and the person's choice at the shown destination.

**The first line and the stamp.** Above the first message: section 3's sentence. Stamps follow the transcript's rule.

**Empty**, right after it is made: the combined avatar at 56 px, the name (20/500), section 3's two sentences, and the composer focused.

## 7. Who answers

The gateway has no "who answers": each eligible target can start or queue its own turn, in its own task (research §1, §3). So the person chooses, Clawtrate chooses the tasks from that before anything is sent, and the composer shows the result. (F-079)

**The rule.** One rule decides who receives a message, and the same resolved set is used for the To line, Send's name and the sends. Its steps, in order of precedence:

1. **A Reply** goes to the bot replied to, and only to it. Nothing in the text adds or removes a target.

Resolve outside quoted lines. A line beginning with `>` after indentation is quoted. Type `@` at the start of a line or after whitespace or an opening bracket or quote. A typed token uses letters, digits, hyphens and underscores and matches a complete member name ignoring case. If an exact name cannot be typed as one token, select it from the list. A list choice binds that token occurrence to an agent id in this draft. A changed or deleted token loses its binding, and sending clears all bindings. Outside a Reply, a reserved `@everyone` takes precedence over other mentions. Otherwise an unresolved duplicate disables Send and lists the matching named bots, and the remaining resolved names choose their eligible members in record order. If there are no matching names use all eligible members. A Reply keeps its one target. If that task cannot take the reply, say why and disable Send until the person cancels Reply or the same task is eligible again. Quote prefixes every inserted line with `>` and leaves a fresh unquoted line for the draft.

Eligible means a member whose task can take a message now: not archived, not gone, not changed (section 11). A member named but not eligible is shown struck through in the To line with its reason, and is not sent to.

| The person writes                          | Sent to                    | What each receives                                                        |
| ------------------------------------------ | -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| No mention                                 | Every eligible member      | The text as typed                                                         |
| `@everyone` anywhere outside a quoted line | Every eligible member      | The text as typed, `@everyone` included                                   |
| `@Otto` (one or more members' names)       | Those members, if eligible | The text as typed, the mentions included                                  |
| A Reply                                    | That bot only              | The text as typed, with the replied-to message given to it by the gateway |

- **A mention is the app's routing.** `chat.send`'s `mentions` name people, not bots, and the gateway refuses them from this client ("Human mentions require a signed-in Control UI chat."). So the app never sends `mentions` and sends the text unchanged (research §5). Each bot receives exactly what the person typed, which the gateway wraps with the time and the sender as it does any message (research §4).
- **The To line** shows the resolved set before anything is sent, then its count and what it costs: "To Everyone · 3 bots · Each bot that answers uses its own tokens"; "To Otto · 1 bot · Each bot that answers uses its own tokens". Every target is a separate run of its own bot. Send's name says the same set: "Send to everyone", "Send to Otto".
- **Reply.** A bot's bubble has "Reply" in its menu (hover shows the menu button; touch and hold on the phone). It shows the reply strip, "Replying to Juniper", and the To line reads "To Juniper (replying)": a reply goes only to the bot that wrote the message, with `replyToId` set to that message's id. Send the assistant message's `__openclaw.id` as `replyToId` to that message's own task. If the gateway can read back the referenced message, use its reply preview. Otherwise show 'Original message unavailable.' Cancel on the strip, or Escape in an empty field, ends the reply.
- **Quote.** A bubble's menu also has "Quote". It puts "> Juniper: " and the first 500 characters of the message, every line prefixed with `>`, at the start of the draft, and leaves the caret on a fresh unquoted line. Mentions inside it choose no one (the rule above). The quoted words are part of the person's message, visible before it is sent, and each bot it goes to receives them as written. This is how a person passes one bot's words to another; a bot can also message another with its own tool (section 3).
- **No turn-taking.** Every target can start or queue its own turn at once, in its own task; the app holds none back for another. Replies arrive in any order and take their saved places (section 6).

## 8. While several bots work

Each member's run is the run `conversation.md` describes, in its own task. The group adds only how several are shown together and what one Send or one Stop does to them.

**Status lines.** One status line per run still active in a member task, stacked at the foot in member order, each the component of `conversation.md` §5 with its avatar, its phrase and its time: "Otto · Reading backup-2026-11-15.log 0:12". A turn the gateway holds in a task's `pendingInputs` has its own line, "Queued by the gateway", until it starts. Each line leaves when its own run ends, so after a mixed finish every run still active keeps its line. Each still opens its own running card.

**The composer during runs.**

| Members                                  | Placeholder                                                                                          | Controls                                                           |
| ---------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| None running                             | "Message the group"                                                                                  | Send                                                               |
| Some running                             | "Message the group"                                                                                  | "Stop 2 bots" (Stop, named for the count of working members), Send |
| A targeted member has a pending question | As above; the shelf row "Otto asked you a question. A message to Otto now is held until you answer." | As above                                                           |
| Stopping                                 | "Message the group"                                                                                  | "Stopping…", Send                                                  |

There is no mode word in a group: each copy goes as a message sent at that moment into that member's task would (`conversation.md` §15), and the order of §15's rules holds: while that member's run is stopping, the copy is queued on this device, whether or not a question is pending; otherwise, with a pending question in that task, it is held; otherwise it is sent, and the gateway starts a run, takes it into the running turn, or queues it (section 6 says which). "Queue after this turn" and "Start as new task" are not offered in a group (section 17).

Stop asks the gateway to stop each active run the group can identify. Capture the current member tasks and active run ids when pressed and send `chat.abort {sessionKey, runId}` for each captured run. Use the same id on retry while that run is still active and its task is still a member. An ordinary connection can stop runs owned by its device or connection. The gateway can also allow an unowned run or an admin request. Name each stopped run only when its own terminal evidence confirms the stop. If two confirmed stops belong to Otto, give each its own line, such as "Otto · Run a36bfcc7 stopped". If it finished first, show "Finished before Stop took effect." If it was stopped elsewhere, use the task's stop note. On "unauthorized" show "Couldn't stop Otto's run from this device." Keep its last confirmed live state until it changes. On no answer show "No answer about Otto's run." A queued input keeps its own state until its own confirmed result. Cancel only held or queued local copies associated with the confirmed stopped run. Update each pending card from its own gateway outcome using the task's approval and question mapping.

Stopping one member alone is done in that member's own task.

## 9. Approvals and questions

Reuse the task cards and their list, replay, and event rules. Place each shown card under its member's speaker line and identify it by task, run and request id when the gateway supplies them. Use the ordinary task attribution when a field is missing. Show and answer a question only when this connection receives or lists it and the gateway allows its resolution. A bound ordinary approval can be answered by its paired reviewer with approval access. An admin connection can also answer when the gateway authorizes it. A confirmed `waiting-approval` with no card shows "Otto is waiting for approval. This connection has no approval card for this run." Use the task card's choices and its recorded outcome. For an ordinary command with Always allow, describe the asking bot's command allowlist. Use the request's own choices for other approval families. (F-080, F-179, F-289, F-290)

## 10. Group details: name, members, removing

The title pill or the row's menu opens **Group details**, a dialog of 440 px (a sheet on the phone). It reads the record first ("Reading the group…"), and every change on it follows one pattern: read the record, describe the tasks the change names, write the whole record with `users.prefs.set` and `expectedEntries` holding the record as read, then read it back. What the dialog shows after a change is the read-back, never what was sent.

Compare the read-back with the requested record after every write outcome, including success, refusal, conflict and timeout. If it matches, show the requested state as read back. If it differs, show the record now saved and offer the edit again against that record. If the group is absent, say "This group is no longer saved." If it cannot be read, say "Couldn't confirm the change." Check again only reads. Retry is a separate action and takes a new pre-read. If a write would now repeat a completed change, show the current record and send nothing.

**Name.** The name (17/500) with "Rename" (desktop) or, on the phone, "Edit" then a name field and "Done" (F-288). Saving shows the field disabled and "Saving…", then the rule above; "Saved the name" is announced. An empty name says "A group needs a name." and Save is disabled.

**Members**, in order, each a row: avatar, name, "Open Otto's task" while the task exists and, for the interactive tier with more than two members, "Remove". Then section 3's sentence. Then "Add a bot" while there are fewer than six: a choice list of the other bots; adding makes the bot's task (`sessions.create`) and then writes the record. When the task was made and the record was not saved, it says from the read-back "Pip's task was made. This group still has Juniper, Otto and Marlow.", and "Try again" writes the record with that same task, without making another. Member order is the record's order; the earliest added is first.

**At two members** there is no Remove for a member anywhere: every place that would offer one (the members list, section 11's lines) offers "Add a bot" and "Remove group…" instead, and the members list says "A group has at least two bots. To change one, add another bot first, or remove the group."

**Kept where.** One line at the foot: "Kept with your preferences on the gateway. Devices signed in to the same gateway profile show this group." (section 14, rows 9 and 10).

**Removing a member** asks first, `.b-dialog` 380, with what the describe found:

> **Remove Marlow from Weekend plans?**
> Marlow's task is present. Removing this group record keeps the tasks and their histories. Work the gateway already accepted can continue.

For a member whose task or bot is gone, the first sentence is "Marlow's task is already gone." or "Marlow was deleted, with its tasks." "Remove" (danger) and "Cancel". Removing rewrites the record without Marlow. After read-back, drop Marlow's task, its replies, its cards and its active runs from the group. A saved outgoing bubble remains only if a current member has a saved copy. Keep a locally held copy until its Not sent outcome has been shown. Marlow's own task keeps its history and any gateway work already accepted. A copy held on this device for Marlow, offline or behind a question, is checked against the members when it would be released and becomes "Not sent to Marlow", "Marlow is no longer in this group."

**Removing the group** asks first, naming only the tasks still present:

> **Remove Weekend plans?**
> Juniper's, Otto's and Marlow's tasks are present. Removing this group record keeps the tasks and their histories. Work the gateway already accepted can continue.

When a member's task is gone, it is listed separately: "Juniper's and Otto's tasks are present. Marlow's task is already gone. Removing this group record keeps the tasks and their histories. Work the gateway already accepted can continue." "Remove group" (danger) and "Cancel". It writes `null` for the record and reads back that it is absent. Then the group leaves the rail and the stage shows "Removed Weekend plans. Juniper's, Otto's and Marlow's tasks stay in their lists." When the record is already absent: "This group is no longer saved." and the same sentence about the tasks.

## 11. A member that is gone, archived or changed

The record keeps each member's task key and the `sessionId` that task had when it was made or adopted (section 4).

Before dispatch or release, read the current members and describe each chosen task. Skip a member removed since the draft or a task which no longer matches its recorded identity. Send each remaining copy with that `sessionId`, `expectedLeafEntryId: null`, and no `queueMode`. Apply the task composer's local stopping and question rules first. For anything sent, use the task's gateway default and label the result using the copy's row, pending input or terminal event. A preflight finds tasks already gone. If a different session is now at the key, say "Marlow's task changed during the send." ## 12. Other devices, read-only, offline

**Another device signed in to the same gateway profile** shows the same groups: the record is that profile's preference, and a change arrives as `users.prefs.changed` (research §7). It builds the column from the task histories: saved messages are ordered by gateway timestamp, each message is drawn once, and its line says in whose task histories its copies were found (section 6). It can stop only the runs it started (section 8). It shows every ordinary question it receives or lists, and answers it; it shows an approval card only when it receives or reads back that card, and otherwise the wait of section 9: "Otto is waiting for approval. This connection has no approval card for this run." An admin connection can also answer approvals (research §9, from source); this design asks for no admin access for it.

**The read tier** shows every group, its column and its live work; the access bar of `conversation.md` §3 takes the composer's place: "**Read-only.** You can follow this group here. Approvals and questions aren't shown on this connection." with "Request access". "+" has no New group, Group details has no Rename, Add or Remove, and the row's menu has only "Group details". A group made on another device arrives on this one too.

**Offline.** A message written while the connection is down is held by the host, as `conversation.md` §6 holds one: one bubble in the raised fill, "Sends when reconnected". When the connection returns, its targets are checked against the members the record has then; it is sent once per target still a member, guarded as section 11 says, with its where-it-went line then, and a former member's copy is "Not sent to Marlow".

**Another person** on the same gateway does not see this person's groups: preferences are per profile (research §7).
